# Generated portraits for the evaluator game

The pictures in `public/portraits/` are generated by the scripts in this folder. They are study stimuli, so the
method is fixed and every step is deterministic or logged. Nothing here is committed until the owner has reviewed it.

## What is delivered

The file names follow `src/ui/portraitImages.ts`. All 160 files are listed in `public/portraits/manifest.json` under
`files`, and every picture is 384 x 384 px:

| Files | Name | Count | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cast | `{slot}-{sex}-{tone}.webp`: slot 0 to 7, sex `woman` or `man`, tone `l1`, `l2`, `d1`, `d2` from lightest to darkest | 64 | 18 to 31 KB |
| One-offs | `o{slot}-{sex}.webp`: slots 100 to 143 (every oneoff beat in `content/story.json`, both sexes), at the constant medium tone | 88 | 20 to 28 KB |
| Mascots | `mascot-{animal}.webp`: bear, panda, frog, koala, elephant, penguin, rabbit, tiger | 8 | 12 to 22 KB |

The `s1` to `s8` used inside this folder are game slots 0 to 7. Every cast slot has both a woman and a man.

Those 160 files are the warehouse story's. Each other story has its own cast of 8, named with the story id from
`content/story.json` first: `newsroom-{slot}-{sex}-{tone}.webp` and `clinic-{slot}-{sex}-{tone}.webp`, 64 files
each, with the same slot, sex and tone codes. The mascots and one-offs are shared. See "Other stories' casts" below.

## Pipeline

| Step | Script | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cast and prompts | `cast.py` | One comic style, one uniform, one backdrop, one skin tone. Each slot has its own face, hair and age, and glasses and facial hair are balanced 2x2 across the slots. One-off characters (slots 100 and up) are read from `content/story.json`. |
| 2. Generate | `generate.py`, `run.sh` | FLUX.2 klein 4B (snapshot `e7b7dc27`), 8-bit through mflux 0.19.1, 4 steps, 512 px. Images already on disk are skipped. Every seed, prompt and timing goes to `work/generation-log.json`. |
| 3. Analyse | `analyse.py` (`backdrop.py`, `skinmask.py`, `frame.py`) | Normalises the backdrop, builds the skin mask, finds the eyes and sets the crop. Writes `work/base`, `work/mask` and `work/overlay`. |
| 4. Rate and pick | `ratings.json`, `pick.py` | Applies provisional ratings, rejects candidates and picks the most closely matched woman/man pair per slot, plus the mascots. Writes `scores.json`. |
| 5. Build | `build.py` (`recolour.py`) | Recolours the skin tones, crops, and exports 384 px WebP at q 90. Writes `public/portraits/manifest.json`. |
| 6. Verify | `check.py` | Checks the pixel differences outside the mask and the tone accuracy. Writes `check-output.txt`. |
| 7. Review | `sheets.py` | Draws the contact sheets. |

Commands, run from the repo root. Generation uses mflux's own Python; everything else uses the system `python3` with
numpy, scipy and Pillow:

```
scripts/portraits/run.sh cast,mascots oneoffs     # about 30 s an image; restarts itself after a GPU timeout
python3 scripts/portraits/analyse.py --force
python3 scripts/portraits/pick.py
python3 scripts/portraits/build.py
python3 scripts/portraits/check.py
python3 scripts/portraits/sheets.py <review-dir>
```

`work/` holds the raw images, masks and intermediates. It is git-ignored, which keeps the raw candidates out of the
repo while P1 is pending.

## Method

**Prompts.** Every person shares one prompt frame. It sets a close-up head-and-shoulders view, an Indian adult, medium brown
skin, a neutral expression with a slight closed-mouth smile, and a straight look. It also asks for a plain deep violet
polo with no pocket and a yellow lanyard with a blank white card. The skin is to be smooth with light linework, the
forehead bare, and there is to be no jewellery, no makeup and no head covering. The style is ink and halftone in flat
colours on a plain pale lavender ground (Direction C). Within a slot the woman's and the man's prompts differ only in
three things: the sex noun, the hairstyle, and facial hair (men only). They also share the seed. One-offs wear a plain
mid-grey collared shirt with nothing round the neck, the same for all of them. The full prompts are in
`work/generation-log.json`.

**Backdrop (`backdrop.py`).** The lavender varies a little between images. In each base, the backdrop pixels that
connect to the image border are shifted to `#DCD0EE`. This happens before anything else, so every tone shares it.

**Skin mask (`skinmask.py`).** The mask is deterministic. It uses no learned model.
1. The reference is the median Lab of plausible skin in the face core.
2. Each pixel gets a soft weight from four ramps: L* above the ink and hair range, chroma/L* like skin, hue within
   9 to 16 degrees of the reference, and chroma of at least 10.
3. Connected regions under 150 px are dropped, and the rest is dilated by 2 px.
4. Inside the face-and-neck silhouette, the rule also takes in dark skin-hued pixels (skin mixed with ink or halftone)
   and small highlights with no ink in them. It uses a wider hue window here, to catch red shadow shades. Within 12 px
   outside the silhouette it takes in skin-hued shade with L* above 34 (a neck shadow that runs into the collar).
5. The holes that hold the eyes and the mouth keep their step-2 weights, so irises, eye whites, lashes and lips are
   never changed. Brows, hair and clothes fall outside the colour rule.

`masks.json` can force ellipses in or out of a mask, or set eye points by hand. No base needed it.

**Recolour (`recolour.py`).** The comic draws flat colour with ink and halftone multiplied over it. Inside the mask,
each pixel is multiplied in linear RGB by one per-channel gain, which takes the base's reference skin colour exactly
to the target. Ratios between pixels survive, so shading, halftone and ink keep their depth, and black stays black. A
soft shoulder above 0.92 linear stops clipping. Partial weights blend the recoloured pixel with the original. A pixel
with weight 0 is copied byte for byte.

The targets (`TONES` in `recolour.py`, the same for every story):

| Tone | Hex now | L* now | Hex until 20 Oct 2026 | L* then |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| l1 | `#EDC6A2` | 82.4 | `#EDC6A2` | 82.4 |
| l2 | `#D09970` | 67.5 | `#D9A47B` | 71.4 |
| d1 | `#A9724B` | 52.9 | `#9A6440` | 47.4 |
| d2 | `#7C4F31` | 38.0 | `#6B4329` | 32.5 |
| m (one-offs only) | `#BC8459` | 59.8 | `#BC8459` | 59.8 |

**Stimulus change, 20 Oct 2026 (owner).** The first targets were the game's tokens (`src/styles/tokens.css`). The
owner found the darkest tone too dark and asked for bigger, more even steps. The old steps in CIELAB L* were 11.0,
24.0 and 14.9: one large jump at the light/dark boundary and a small one inside the light pair. The new targets keep
l1, raise d2 by 5.5 L*, and space the four tones evenly, 14.9, 14.6 and 14.9 L* apart. a* and b* for each new L* are
interpolated along the old five tokens (m included), so hue and chroma follow the old curve. The one-offs' constant m
(L* 59.8) now sits near the middle of the scale, 7.7 below l2 and 6.9 above d1. The light pair (L* 67.5 and up) and
the dark pair (52.9 and down) stay 14.6 L* apart, so the cue still has two distinct sides, though the gap between
them is smaller than the old 24.0. The bases and masks did not change. Only the recolour target changed, so l1 files and
one-offs and mascots were byte-identical to before. (Mask step 5, added afterwards, then changed every warehouse cast
file. See Verification.) The tokens in `src/styles/tokens.css` (the drawn-SVG fallback)
still hold the old values and should be updated to match. Before-and-after sheet: `tones-before-after.png` in the
warehouse review folder.

The base itself (L* about 57 to 72) is never shipped. Every delivered person went through the same recolour.

**Framing (`frame.py`).** The generator draws some people bigger in the frame than others. Each base is therefore
cropped to a square 5.2 eye-distances wide, with the midpoint of the eyes at (50%, 38%). Eye centres come from the
whites of the eyes, merged per eye. Where the box would leave the image, it is padded with the backdrop colour, unless
hair or clothing touches that edge. In that case the box is shifted back inside: s4-w-c0 by 10.7 px, s1-w-c1 by 13.4
px and a few others by up to 2.6 px, all logged in `work/analysis.json` as `crop_shift`. The crop belongs to the base,
so all four tones of a person are pixel-aligned.

**Matching (`ratings.json`, `pick.py`).** The ratings are PROVISIONAL: one rater (the generating agent) scored every
candidate from the candidate sheets at the base tone. The scores are apparent age in years, and attractiveness, warmth
and competence on 1 to 7. They are not P1 data.

The distance between a woman and a man candidate is |age gap|/3 + |attractiveness| + |warmth| + |competence|. Ties go
to the smaller gap in a crude linework proxy: the share of near-black pixels inside the face. A pair is flagged when
the distance is 1 or more, the age gap is 3 years or more, or the linework gap is over 0.03. After P1 re-rates, change
`ratings.json` and re-run `pick.py`, `build.py` and `check.py`.

Rejected candidates are kept and rated, so P1 can rate them too. The generator added a forehead dot that reads as a
bindi to 5 of the 24 women candidates (s1-w-c2, s4-w-c2, s7-w-c0, s7-w-c1, s8-w-c0) and to none of the men. All five
were rejected as attire marking religion.

**One-offs.** The slot order follows the order of the oneoff beats in `content/story.json`, as `session.ts` reads it.
A rank beat gives a caste pair, then a birthplace pair, with the focal member first in each. A pick beat gives one
pair. A split beat gives a third person, then a pair. The game draws the sex per player (`sexDraw`), so every slot has
both sexes. As the brief asked, there is one candidate per slot and sex. To keep the two members of a tied pair
alike, each pair shares an age, glasses and facial hair (men), while its members differ in face and hairstyle.

Three first draws (o133-w, o139-w and o142-w) had a dot between the brows that reads as a bindi. Each was re-rolled
once with seed + 1. The first draws are kept in `work/rejected/`. The one-offs carry provisional ratings too, and
`pick.py` flags every tied pair whose members differ noticeably.

**Mascots.** One prompt frame was used for all 8 animals, with 2 seeds each. The picks were made by eye, for the same
framing and a similar level of detail: bear c0, panda c1, koala c0, elephant c1 and rabbit c0. In a second round (owner's request), the
same frame plus a bust sentence (`BUST` in `cast.py`) gave 3 more candidates each for the tiger, penguin and frog,
which had come out head-only or without a neck. The picks were tiger c3, frog c2 and penguin c3.

## Verification

`check.py` runs four checks, and its output is in `check-output.txt`:

1. **Outside the mask at 512 px**, every variant must be byte-identical to its base, so max |diff| must be 0.
2. **Outside the resampling footprint at 384 px**, before WebP, the same must hold. The footprint is the mask dilated
   by 4 px and carried through the same Lanczos resize.
3. **After WebP**, the same comparison is reported but not required: lossy encoding adds small differences everywhere.
4. **Tone accuracy** compares the median Lab of the mask core in each variant with its target (Delta E 1976).

Every variant was also looked at, in `mask-check.png` and `pairs-tones.png`, for halos and for bleed into the lips
and eyes. The first build showed three defects:
- a pale nose-tip shine on the darkest tone;
- light, salmon-coloured neck shadows on the darkest tone;
- a light ear edge.

The mask refinement in step 4 fixed all three. After the 20 Oct tone change, every darkest variant was looked at
again in close-ups of the face and neck. The faint salmon hatching under the jaw of 5-woman and 7-woman was still there, so the
owner had mask step 5 (`skinmask.py`, red-side neck shade) applied to the warehouse cast too. That removed it. It
changed the masks and so all 64 warehouse cast files. One-offs and mascots are untouched, and all 152 variants still
pass the exact checks.

**Results (`check-output.txt`).**
- All 152 person variants pass both exact checks: max |diff| is 0 outside the mask at 512 px and 0 outside the
  footprint at 384 px.
- After WebP, the mean |diff| outside the footprint is 0.65 to 1.65 levels, with isolated maxima up to 121 on
  halftone dots.
- The core median lands within Delta E 1.45 of its target (median 0.36).

**Compute.** Generation took about 80 minutes for the 152 images and 3 re-rolls, plus about 10 minutes of pilots, on
an Apple M4 with 24 GB. That is about 30 s an image once `mx.clear_cache()` runs between images. Without it, MLX's
cache drove the Mac into swap at about 95 s an image, and one run hit a GPU timeout. Analysis, build and checks take
under a minute.

## What could not be controlled

- **Lips keep the base colour** at every tone, as the method requires. On d2 they read lighter and pinker than the
  skin, and on l1 darker. That is a lip-to-skin contrast that varies with tone.
- **Faces drawn by sex.** The model draws men with more ink (forehead and nasolabial lines) and women smoother and
  younger-looking. The ratings show this, and slots s6 and s7 are flagged for it. Hair necessarily differs by sex.
  Facial hair exists only on men, in 4 of the 8 slots.
- **Salmon hatching under some jaws** survived on d2 in the first build (5-woman, 7-woman). Mask step 5 removed it
  on 20 Oct 2026. In `necks-d2.png` none is visible now.
- **Head size** is normalised on eye distance. Drawn proportions still vary, for example rounder faces and wider-set
  eyes.
- **Mascot appeal is not equal.** The tiger is still the most colourful and detailed (orange with stripes). The
  penguin shows a shoulder line at the flippers but no true neck, which no candidate drew. The koala has a faint collar line. The panda,
  koala and rabbit read as the cutest.
- **One-off tied pairs were not picked for a match.** Each one has a single draw, and 24 of the 40 same-sex tied pairs
  are flagged in `scores.json`. The clearest gaps come from the generator drawing some faces rounder and older
  (o100-m, o107, o139, o124) and from long, loose, wavy hair reading as more glamorous (o103-w, o111-w, o119-w,
  o127-w, o135-w, o143-w). In `session.ts` the focal member always takes the lower slot of its pair, so each face is
  tied to one side of the caste or birthplace cue for every player. The pairs need matching, through extra candidates
  or P1 ratings, or the game has to swap the two images of a pair at random per player.
- **Cast sex by slot in the code.** `drawCast` in `session.ts` currently gives slots 0 to 3 to women and 4 to 7 to
  men, and pairs each woman with a random man as her rival. The brief assumed sex is drawn per slot. Both sexes are
  delivered for every slot, but the matched woman/man pair in a slot only works as a control if the code draws sex per
  slot, or pairs rivals by slot.
- The generator is not bit-reproducible across machines or mflux versions. The raw images in `work/raw` are the
  record.

## Other stories' casts (newsroom, clinic)

Owner, 20 Oct 2026: each story gets its own cast of 8, working towards about 10 stories. The newsroom and the clinic
are done this way. Everything above applies unchanged, except for the points below.

**What differs.**
- **Uniform.** One per story, set in `STORY_CASTS` in `cast.py`. The newsroom wears a plain mid-blue button-up shirt
  with a green lanyard and a blank white press card. The clinic wears a plain teal scrubs top with a navy lanyard and
  a blank white ID badge. Scrubs were used instead of a white coat because a coat reads as "doctor" and scrubs do not,
  so the uniform does not cue a role. There is no stethoscope for the same reason.
- **Looks.** Each story has its own ages (28 to 39), face shapes and hairstyles. Glasses and facial hair are again
  balanced 2x2 across the slots, in a different arrangement in each story. Facial hair is 2 moustaches and 2 short
  beards. The woman and the man of a slot share the prompt, glasses and seed. The frame, style, backdrop, base tone,
  expression and 3 candidates per slot and sex are the warehouse's.
- **Matching.** Matching uses apparent age and attractiveness only: distance = |age gap|/3 + |attractiveness gap|,
  with the same flag rules (`ratings-<story>.json`, `scores-<story>.json`). The ratings are PROVISIONAL, from one
  rater (the generating agent).
- **Re-rolls.** A candidate with a forehead dot that reads as a bindi, or facial hair its slot does not have, was
  re-drawn with seed + 100 (`STORY_RESEED`). The first draws are kept in `work/stories/<story>/rejected/` and logged
  under `rejected` in that story's `generation-log.json`. Newsroom: s2-w-c0, s7-w-c1 and s7-w-c2 (dot), and s5-m-c2
  (a faint moustache). Clinic: s8-w-c0 (dot). Every re-draw came out clean. Five clinic men drew stippled stubble on
  the upper lip in a clean-shaven slot (s2-m-c0, s7-m-c0, s7-m-c2, s8-m-c1, s8-m-c2). They were rejected, not
  re-drawn, because each slot kept at least one clean candidate.
- **Mask.** These draws have redder neck shadows than the warehouse's. Step 4 left them as salmon halftone on d2, so
  `skinmask.py` step 5 adds red-side shade under the chin. It now applies to every story's cast, the warehouse
  included, but not to the one-offs.

**Commands.** These are the same scripts, with the story in `PORTRAIT_STORY` (`story.py`). Each story works in
`work/stories/<story>/` and writes `check-output-<story>.txt`:

```
PORTRAIT_STORY=newsroom scripts/portraits/run.sh cast
PORTRAIT_STORY=newsroom python3 scripts/portraits/analyse.py
PORTRAIT_STORY=newsroom python3 scripts/portraits/pick.py
PORTRAIT_STORY=newsroom python3 scripts/portraits/build.py
PORTRAIT_STORY=newsroom python3 scripts/portraits/check.py
PORTRAIT_STORY=newsroom python3 scripts/portraits/sheets.py <review-dir>/newsroom
```

`build.py` rebuilds `manifest.json` from every story's `build.json`. Building one story therefore never drops another
story's files. To add a story, add it to `STORIES` in `story.py` and to `STORY_CASTS` in `cast.py`.

**Results.**
- Both stories: 64 variants each, and all pass both exact checks (max |diff| 0 outside the mask at 512 px and outside
  the footprint at 384 px).
- Tone accuracy is within Delta E 1.78 for the newsroom (median 0.40) and 1.47 for the clinic (median 0.54).
- Files are 17 to 28 KB.
- Generation took about 25 s an image: 96 candidates plus 5 re-rolls.

**Flags (provisional ratings).**
- Newsroom s6 (game slot 5): every woman candidate was drawn about 38 to 40 and plainer. The men were drawn about 33
  to 34. The best pair is 4 years and 1 attractiveness point apart.
- Linework gaps, with the man inkier: newsroom s1 and s3, and clinic s2 and s4.
- Clinic s6-w-c1 (game slot 5) has pink cheeks that read as blush at l1 and fade at the darker tones. That is a
  tone-dependent cue like the lips.
- Small remaining quirks:
  - green glints on some lenses (clinic s3-m-c0);
  - a reddish shadow on the temple and jaw of clinic s3-m-c2 (not picked) and s8-w-c2;
  - a grey-green cast around the mouth of newsroom s5-m-c1.
- Newsroom s1-w (bob) and s3-w (shoulder-length, straight) have similar hairstyles.

### Looking up a story's cast (for `Portrait`)

A cast file is `{prefix}{slot}-{sex}-{tone}.webp`. The prefix is empty for the warehouse and `{storyId}-` for every
other story, where `storyId` is the story's `id` in `content/story.json` (`newsroom`, `clinic`). `manifest.json`
spells this out under `casts` (`{ "newsroom": { "prefix": "newsroom-", "pattern": "newsroom-{slot}-{sex}-{tone}.webp",
"slots": 8 }, ... }`). The current reader reads only `files` and ignores `casts`.

So `fileFor` needs the current story: a `story` on the cast `Look`, or a parameter. For a cast member it should build
`${story === 'warehouse' ? '' : story + '-'}${slot}-${sex}-${tone}.webp`. As now, it should use only names listed in
`files`. If a story's file is missing, draw the SVG. Never fall back to the warehouse face, because that would show
one story's colleague in another story. One-offs (`o{slot}-{sex}.webp`) and mascots are shared by all stories and
need no prefix.
