# Retake: feature set

## How to read this

- **A row** is one thing a player gets or can do, or one thing the game deliberately never does. It names where it lives, who needs it (the personas P1 to P6 in [personas.md](personas.md)) and when it ships.
- **First version?** **V1** is needed for the first release the owner puts in front of players. **Later** is designed after V1. **Never** is a deliberate non-goal, listed again with its reason under "Never".
- **Basis** points to journey stages S1 to S15 and moments M1 to M5 in [journeys.md](journeys.md), rule ids (R-...) in [the consolidated rules](../reference/design-rules-consolidated.md), the owner's decisions O1 to O17 (O11 Qn is the owner's answer to question n of 21 Oct 2026; O12 is the starter and difficulty decision of the same day; O13 is the game's name, Retake, with the starter's screen names; O16 is the owner's review of the First take draft, 22 Oct 2026, and "O16 (answer n)" names its answer n; O17 is the second round on the rewritten First take, the same day, cited the same way; D1 to D9 are the defaults set with the O11 answers and D10 to D18 those set with O12, all listed under "Owner answers"), sections of [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) and [PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md) (PROD), digest ids (DG, UA, HIG, UXW, PH), and the journeys' old question numbers (OQ-n). The critique is [critique-stage1.md](critique-stage1.md).
- **Status.** **Decided** means settled by an owner decision, a binding rule or DESIGN.md, or by a default this file sets for an old question not worth the owner's time now (Basis names the default). **Decided (default, owner to confirm)** means the row rests in part on one of the defaults D1 to D18 set with the owner's answers and decisions of 21 Oct 2026; it stands unless the owner says otherwise. No row waits on a question. **From the research line** in Basis marks a part the research line still owes (listed under "Owner answers"); the row stands and only that part waits. **Open** means the owner has left it open and it is not asked now; the tag shown is where it would sit if it ever comes.
- **Conflicts.** The rules file's order holds: the owner, then the rules, then the digests. Where the fortnight content plan or the drawn library breaks a rule, the rule wins and Basis says so.
- **Not asked again:** the look-and-feel choices already open in DESIGN.md "Open owner choices". Their defaults are used here.

## In one line

A daily puzzle that shows you your two ways: open a link, play a short puzzle at your level on a printed object, see your score and the size of your lean, share a card that is safe in a family group, and on Sunday look again at where you judged the same thing two ways. Or start with the First take, an optional 20-minute starter whose private results show how your reading was made, and take it again each month as the Next take.

## Never

- **Phone numbers, passwords, or a sign-up wall before the first puzzle (F-14).** Scams are a top worry, and EVAL-13 lost every persona in its first three screens. The one screen before the first puzzle is the birth date; the email login comes after the first result and is never needed to play.
- **Showing which way a player leans (F-60), outside the starter's own results.** In the daily game the player works it out. A direction turns reflection into a verdict, and into a clip for a hostile screenshotter. The owner allows one exception (O12): the starter's results show it privately, on that screen only, never shared, to help people grow.
- **The word "bias" and research words on screen (F-61).** They read as judgement, or as a test to play to. The starter too is never called a test, or about attitude or bias.
- **A difficulty number on the cover, or the day's trick named on a daily (F-07).** "Level" now means Easy, Medium or Hard, so a second number would confuse, and a named trick is one to play to; the starter's results are the one place the method is explained.
- **Timers, countdowns and pop-up alerts (F-13).** Players are interrupted all the time, and no timers is the owner's rule.
- **Streak-loss countdowns (F-76), guilt in reminders and badge counts (F-132).** Pressure teaches people to protect a number, not to look at their choices.
- **Bottom lists, rank-drop notices and prizes worth money (F-95).** Shame drives players away and money invites gaming the board.
- **Notices about friends' play (F-96).** A player can drift off without anyone being told.
- **Typed road names, real names, photos or places (F-94).** They invite slurs and expose real people.
- **A switch to hide from Everyone, or a wait before new players appear (F-89a).** Owner, O11 Q4: everyone who joins appears on Everyone; who sees a player's practice is limited instead by friends being mutual (F-85a).
- **Getting days back by typing a road name (F-172a).** Road names are public, so anyone could type someone else's and take their days; the email code does this instead.
- **Ads, prices, paywalls and purchases (F-15).** It is a free game, and newly online players read a price as a paywall.
- **Per-person reports for HR, colleges or facilitators (F-104).** That turns play into compliance, and compliance brings pushback.
- **Keeping any reflection tap answer, on Sunday, in the month or later (F-77).** That would be new data the privacy exception does not cover.
- **A "?" line naming the lean's side and the repeated records (F-110), while DESIGN's default A holds.** Default A never names the trick in the daily game, and the starter's results (F-203) are the one place it is explained; the line comes in only if the owner picks B.
- **A confirm on leaving, a separate "Next", or the same answer asked twice (F-49).** Nothing is lost on leaving, and asking twice was an EVAL-13 failure.
- **A light or dark switch, or a locked orientation (F-151).** Each world is one fixed look, and a locked orientation fails players who turn their phone.
- **Puzzles that need sound or a voice note (F-160).** A household can hear them, and a train makes them useless.
- **Saving tomorrow's puzzle on the phone (F-173).** It could be read and shared before its day.
- **The Equal Lens logo, colours or mascot in play (F-121).** The owner wants a small credit only.
- **Study groups, study screens or a date override in the player build (F-122).** The research runs in its own build, and players never see it.
- **Analytics, trackers, anything fetched from other sites, device fingerprints, location, or IP addresses kept longer than delivery needs (F-122b).** The brief's privacy terms block them, and each tells someone else who plays.
