## The story in one breath

**The household:**
- Nani, turning eighty, who runs everything from where she sits;
- Mummy, 52, who runs the family's electrical shop facing the platform;
- Papa, 56, who books every train months early;
- Mama ji, 47;
- Tinku, 21, the lane team's scorer.

You are the grown-up child everyone phones. On New Year's morning Mummy hangs the shop's free calendar and the year's days get circled.

**The chapters:**
1. Fourteen people, ten berths: the overnight train to Nani's eightieth, safety, Lugdi look.
2. One night in the ward: Nani slipped on the wet back step, home, Rukavat look.
3. The week nobody circled: the monsoon floods the shop, work, Station look.
4. The lane cup on 15 August, where the losing lane clears the broken drain: teams, Cricket look.
5. Sixty guests, one lane: a cousin's wedding at Nani's house, trust, Jadugar look.

## What works

- **The opening places you in about 40 words:** Nani's "Everyone comes", Mummy's free calendar from the shop, "Everyone calls you anyway", the pen.
- **Being the grown-up child everyone phones works for every player**, the 61-year-old included.
- **One chapter leads to the next all year.**
- **The outcomes are clean.** Swapping the hidden pair, or always picking the woman or always the man, never changes an ending.
- **The basics hold:**
  - no Hindi line to the player marks gender;
  - the household never sits on a card;
  - no card name repeats across chapters.

## The six big problems

1. **Too long.** About 150 new paintings, 25 to 32 minutes against 20, and about 2,260 counted words on screen in English.
2. **The numbers give the pair away.** In 38 of 40 decisions the hidden pair are the only two cards with matching numbers. With 10 people on fixed numbers, the two left over are always a woman and a man.
3. **Asks nobody would make.** The family shop hands its cash drawer to applicants hired that week, and Nani's spare key goes to neighbours.
4. **The cards read like a poster.** Against-type jobs sit on nearly every card, chapter after chapter.
5. **The strip shows a clock, not people.** Each asker is seen from behind, and most "it happened" beats are empty rooms.
6. **Chapter 4's logic breaks.** Players are in two matches at once, and "Our side, all ten" is followed by picking three more.

## What the story says about the rules

- **"The pair is never the only tie" cannot hold with circles of 10 on fixed numbers.** Chapters 2, 4 and 5 broke it on every decision. The options are bigger circles, numbers that change per decision, or dropping the rule.
- **The 20 minutes and 8 decisions a chapter cannot both hold in a strip.** The options are six decisions a chapter, one painting per scene, or accepting about 25 minutes.
- **"The household is painted only in today's style" empties the strip.** Painting each asker face-on in the chapter's look brings the people back. This is open question 11.
- **Rule 5 and rule 17 caught real problems** and are worth keeping.

## Questions this raises for the owner

1. Paint the person asking face-on in each chapter's look?
2. Shorter (6 decisions a chapter, about 20 minutes), or keep 8 and accept about 25?
3. For the numbers: 12 people a chapter, numbers that change per decision, or drop "never the only tie"?
4. Mostly ordinary jobs on the cards, with against-type only now and then?
