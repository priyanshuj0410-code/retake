# Saal Bhar: the cast map

**What this is.** The map the character files will be written from. It fixes who each person is, what they want, how they stand to each other, and what makes each one impossible to confuse with the rest. It does not write their voice files or model sheets; those come next, from the template in [method.md](method.md) section 7.

**Where it comes from.** The draft bible and five chapters (`content/drafts/saal-bhar-2026-10-26/`), the players' and judges' review (`knowledge/story/saal-bhar-draft-2026-10-26.md`), the rulebook with the owner's answers of 25 and 26 Oct, the realism notes ([realism.md](realism.md)) and the method notes ([method.md](method.md)). Anything not traced to one of those is team judgement and is marked as a proposal. The owner decides every line.

**The owner's words this answers (26 Oct).** "Define each character in depth, how they would talk, how they would type, the words they'll use, the clothes they'll wear, their postures everything." "There's no cap on number of characters... just make them real and memorable." "It should be entirely story driven."

## 2. The family, with dates that add up

All places stay unnamed (rule 3: no community-marked place). Three places carry the year: **Nani's town** (an older small town, one night by train), **the junction town** (the household's home, the shop, the lane, the station road) and **Mama ji's city**.

```
           ran a grocery from her front room in her own town, 1972 to 2016

           MAUSI, 55           MUMMY, 52             MAMA JI, 47
           b. 1971             b. 1974               b. 1979
        m. 1992 MAUSA JI, 58   m. 1997 PAPA, 56      m. 2001 MAMI, 45
        (lineman, retired)     (railway parcel       (city hospital
        lives near the          clerk)                billing desk)
        junction; two grown         |                     |
        children, about 32    +-----+-----+          one daughter, 24
        and 29                YOU,       TINKU, 21    (the bride; never
                              22 to 30   (b. 2005)    drawn, named or
                                                      on a card)
   NEIGHBOURS in the junction town: MASTERJI, about 60, the tailor who shares Mummy's wall
   since 1999; CAPTAIN SAHAB, about 65, retired bus-depot mechanic, the lane's old club captain.
```

- **Nana (owner's question).** Default here: he died some years ago, off screen, and is never a plot device. Nothing on screen marks it: Nani never wears white (with the forced bare forehead it reads as a Hindu widow, realism note on dress), and there is no garlanded photograph. Mummy wears his old wristwatch.
- **Mummy's shop**: opened in 1999 on the station road, facing the platform, with stock money Nani lent her; Mummy paid it back in instalments and keeps the last receipt.
- **Papa's work**: the parcel office across that same platform, four years from retiring.
- **Mausi**: has run a tuition class for Classes 1 to 5 in her front room for thirty years. She brought Papa's marriage proposal in 1996 and mentions it at every wedding.
- **Mama ji**: left Nani's town at twenty for the city; sells water pumps to builders; sends Mummy stock leads she did not ask for.
- **Tinku**: final-year commerce student at the town college; works the shop counter on Saturdays; the lane team's scorer since she was twelve.
- **Masterji**: apprenticed at fourteen; his own shop on the station road since 1988.
- **Captain sahab**: 36 years a mechanic at the state bus depot; captained the town's club side through the 1990s; went to one state trial in 1979, aged 18, and was given out wrongly (he says).

**Nani's line looks like one family.** Nani, Mausi, Mummy, Mama ji and Tinku share one feature the painter can hold: thick, straight, dark eyebrows (Nani's now white). The three who married in (Papa, Mausa ji, Mami) do not have it. Skin tones differ across the family, as real families do (section 6).

## 3. The eleven, one at a time

Each block: the label for first use (rule 65), essence, the want this year, the want from you, the fear, the contradiction (said as "X, but Y"), the year from January to December, the line people quote back or the speech habit, flat or round, and which of the six players would say "I know this person".

### Nani (नानी), turning 80
- **Label:** "Nani, Mummy's mother" | "नानी, मम्मी की माँ".
- **Essence:** the lane's old grocer, who still runs her family and her lane from the chair where her counter used to be.
- **Wants this year:** everyone under her roof at once for her eightieth, every head counted.
- **Wants from you:** the plain truth, unsoftened, and someone to hold her list when she cannot.
- **Fears:** being moved out of her own house into a child's flat, a guest in someone else's kitchen. The fall in May makes it real.
- **Contradiction:** she runs everyone's life, but will not let anyone run hers. Every time she is helped, she turns the help into an order she gave ("Use them").
- **Her year:** January, she orders "Everyone comes." May, she slips on her wet back step and runs the ward night from the bed to prove she is fine. December, she sits in the front row of her own house and lets the cake be carried to her. The album: "Eighty. Everyone came."
- **Habit:** counts heads aloud with one finger: "Ek, do, teen... sab?" **Quoted line:** "Sab aayein." | "सब आएँ।" | "Everyone comes."
- **Round.** **I know this person:** Mr Natarajan (his own mother) and Sunita (the elder of her lane).

### Mummy (मम्मी), 52
- **Label:** "Mummy" | "मम्मी" (no label needed; checked by the South Indian reader).
- **Essence:** the electrical-shop owner who runs the family like her shop: everything has a time, a price and a place, and she knows all three.
- **Wants this year:** the shutter up at nine every Saturday, flood or no flood, without asking anyone for help.
- **Wants from you:** to take half the phone calls, which she will never ask for outright. Handing you the red pen is how she asks.
- **Fears:** the shutter staying down: losing the shop that Nani's money started.
- **Contradiction:** she swore she would never sit behind a counter like her mother, but runs hers exactly like her, down to the head-count with one finger.
- **Her year:** January, she hangs the shop's free calendar and holds out the pen. July, the flood makes her hire strangers and take the lane's help. December, at the wedding, she sits down beside Nani for once and lets others serve.
- **Habit:** answers any question with a time or a price. **Quoted line:** "Nau baje. Shutter up." | "नौ बजे। शटर ऊपर।" | "Nine o'clock. Shutter up."
- **Round.** **I know this person:** Ravi (his boss, or his mother).

### Papa (पापा), 56
- **Label:** "Papa" | "पापा".
- **Essence:** the railway parcel clerk who loves in timetables: he cannot say he missed you, so he books you the lower berth.
- **Wants this year:** all of them at Nani's door at 6:00 for her eightieth, and to be counted as one of hers.
- **Wants from you:** to be the one who never needs reminding.
- **Fears:** being late when it matters; and under it, that in the family he married into he is only as welcome as he is useful.
- **Contradiction:** he is exact to the minute about time, but cannot say one plain thing he feels; every feeling comes out as a number.
- **His year:** January, "Trains: booked." May, his phone dies on the night train and he hands you his list, the first list he has ever let go of. He never takes it back.
- **Habit:** corrects any time to the minute. **Quoted line:** "9:40. 9:45 nahin." | "9:40, 9:45 नहीं।" | "9:40, not 9:45."
- **Round.** **I know this person:** Mr Natarajan (himself).

### Mama ji (मामा जी), 47
- **Label:** "Mama ji, Mummy's brother" | "मामा जी, मम्मी के भाई" (rule 65: "Mama" can read as "mother" in English).
- **Essence:** the youngest, who left for the city at twenty and loves his family by phone: generous, worried, and always making the number bigger.
- **Wants this year:** his daughter married from Nani's house, with the whole family there, bigger than anyone expects.
- **Wants from you:** to hear "Sorted, Mama ji" so he can stop worrying. You are his eyes in Nani's town.
- **Fears:** that Nani grew old while he was on the phone in the city; that he is the son who was not there. In May, he is not.
- **Contradiction:** he worries about everything, but plans nothing: he makes every number bigger and leaves the how to others.
- **His year:** January, he types Nani's words back to her ("Everyone comes"). May, he hears of the fall in the city and wants Nani moved into his flat; she refuses. December, he brings the whole wedding to her house instead: he comes to her.
- **Habit:** raises every number by ten. **Quoted line:** "Saath? Sattar kar do!" | "साठ? सत्तर कर दो!" | "Sixty? Make it seventy!"
- **Round.** **I know this person:** Shruti (who left for the metro; her uncle, or herself in twenty years).

### Tinku (टिंकू), 21
- **Label:** "Tinku, your sister" | "टिंकू, आपकी बहन" (the chapter 1 check: "the youngest" is not a relation; this still gives you no gender).
- **Essence:** the family's scorer, who keeps the tally of everything (runs, promises, who said what in which year) and is quietly scoring her way out.
- **Wants this year:** to be more than "the youngest": a place on the district cricket association's scorers' course.
- **Wants from you:** to be treated as an equal; on cup day she hands you the decisions to see what you do with them.
- **Fears:** being "the youngest" for ever, left in the junction town while everyone calls you.
- **Contradiction:** she keeps exact score of everyone else's game, but never writes down her own.
- **Her year:** January, "Lane cup. I'm scoring." August, she runs the cup's book and Captain sahab says the district should see it. December, the course letter has come (painted blank, read as live text).
- **Habit:** turns everything into a tally. **Quoted line:** "Teesri baar. Teen-zero." | "तीसरी बार। तीन-शून्य।" | "Third time. Three-nil."
- **Round.** An asker, so never the comic one (rule 37): she teases, she is never the joke. **I know this person:** Meena (quiet skill with numbers).

### Mausi (मौसी), about 55, chapter 1's voice
- **Label:** "Mausi, Mummy's elder sister" | "मौसी, मम्मी की बड़ी बहन".
- **Essence:** the eldest sister, who has run a tuition class from her front room for thirty years and talks to every room as if it were Class 3.
- **Wants this year:** to feed all fourteen on the night train from her basket and be the first one Nani thanks.
- **Wants from you:** your news first-hand, not through Mummy.
- **Fears:** that she, the eldest, is the daughter Nani tolerates, while Mummy is the one Nani relies on.
- **Contradiction:** she never lets you finish a sentence, but remembers every word of it a year later.
- **Her chapter:** boards loud at 9:40 pm with too many bags, feeds everyone, and quotes back what you told her last year.
- **Habit:** says everything three times. **Quoted line:** "Suno, suno, suno!" | "सुनो, सुनो, सुनो!" | "Listen, listen, listen!"
- **Flat by design** (one chapter, instantly recognisable), with one contradiction. Her talking over people is care, never gossip about anyone's character, and she is the first to believe a report (rule 22). **I know this person:** Sunita.

### Mausa ji (मौसा जी), about 58, chapter 2's voice
- **Label:** "Mausa ji, Mausi's husband" | "मौसा जी, मौसी के पति".
- **Essence:** a retired lineman with big scarred hands who says two blunt words and does twenty kind things.
- **Wants this year:** a quiet first year of retirement, useful and unthanked.
- **Wants from you:** that you sleep while he sits up.
- **Fears:** that his tongue undoes what his hands do, so he keeps it to two words.
- **Contradiction:** he is blunt in words, but soft in deeds: he calls a man lazy, then peels an orange and hands you half.
- **His chapter:** sits the whole ward night by the door, peels oranges for everyone, stands when the doctor passes.
- **Habit:** one word, then an action. **Quoted line:** "Hmm. Ho jayega." | "हम्म। हो जाएगा।" | "Hmm. It'll get done."
- **Flat by design.** Never the silent patriarch: his few words are followed by something he does. **I know this person:** Farida (the man who sits outside her ward all night).

### Masterji (मास्टरजी), about 60, chapter 3's voice
- **Label:** "Masterji, the tailor next door" | "मास्टरजी, बगल वाले दर्ज़ी" (the continuity fix; the South Indian reader checks it, since मास्टरजी often means a teacher).
- **Essence:** the tailor next door, who measures everything (cloth, mud, people, weather) and has an opinion on every inch.
- **Wants this year:** the drain mended before another monsoon ruins a bolt of cloth.
- **Wants from you:** a listener who does not walk off.
- **Fears:** that the station road forgets him: ready-made shops, and a drain that floods his cloth every July.
- **Contradiction:** he is precise to an eighth of an inch, but never on time for anything, his own shutter included. He opens at ten and calls Mummy's nine o'clock "showing off".
- **His chapter:** stands in his flooded doorway with an opinion on the mud, and ends it by handing the drain to the cup: "Let the lane cup decide."
- **Habit:** speaks in measurements. **Quoted line:** "Do inch ka farq." | "दो इंच का फ़र्क़।" | "Two inches out."
- **Flat by design.** His views are about cloth, cut, weather and time, never a woman's dress or morals. **I know this person:** Sunita, herself a tailor, so he must be dignified, never a joke.

### Captain sahab (कप्तान साहब), about 65, chapter 4's voice
- **Label:** "Captain sahab, the lane's old captain" | "कप्तान साहब, गली के पुराने कप्तान".
- **Essence:** the lane's old club captain, a retired bus mechanic who still stands side-on as if waiting for the next ball.
- **Wants this year:** one more lane cup, and someone to hand the lane's scorebook and team to.
- **Wants from you:** a straight bat and a fair pick.
- **Fears:** that the lane cup dies with him and the lane ground becomes parking.
- **Contradiction:** he is the strictest umpire in the lane, but still sore about one decision against him in 1979.
- **His chapter:** sets the stakes (the losing lane mends the drain), umpires strictly, and sees Tinku's book.
- **Habit:** one-line verdicts in cricket calls. **Quoted line:** "Seedha bat. Front foot." | "सीधा बैट। फ़्रंट फ़ुट।" | "Straight bat. Front foot."
- **Flat by design.** **I know this person:** Ravi (the uncle who ran his gully team).

### Mami (मामी), about 45, chapter 5's voice
- **Label:** "Mami, Mama ji's wife" | "मामी, मामा जी की पत्नी" (the chapter 5 check: a South Indian reader checks whether "maami" marks a caste for a Tamil player).
- **Essence:** the manager of a city hospital's billing desk, who runs her daughter's wedding off a clipboard, item by numbered item.
- **Wants this year:** a wedding with no surprises: every envelope, key and plate accounted for.
- **Wants from you:** a second pair of hands she need not explain things to twice.
- **Fears:** something going wrong in her husband's family's house, in front of everyone, when she is the one from outside.
- **Contradiction:** she plans every minute, but cries only at the one thing that was not on her list (Nani's cake).
- **Her chapter:** holds the clipboard out to you ("Then you run my list"), runs the week, and is the only one who cries at the cake.
- **Habit:** numbers her sentences. **Quoted line:** "Pehla, chaabiyan. Doosra, lifafe." | "पहला, चाबियाँ। दूसरा, लिफ़ाफ़े।" | "One, keys. Two, envelopes."
- **Flat by design.** She and Mausi are allies, never women set against women (realism, caricature traps). **I know this person:** Farida (the hospital desk) and Shruti (the lists).

### You (the player, a voice only)
- **Label:** "You" | "आप". Never painted: no face, hand, sleeve or shadow (rule 7). No name, gender, caste or religion (rule 8).
- **Essence:** the grown-up child everyone phones: the family's switchboard, who speaks to each of them in their own language.
- **The story's want for you:** to get everyone through the year without leaving anyone off the list. What the player wants for themselves stays their own.
- **The story's fear for you:** the one person left off the list.
- **Contradiction:** everyone calls you, but nobody asks how you are, until the album, where Nani counts heads and counts you last (section 7).
- **Your object:** the red pen Mummy holds out in January, which circles the year. It is painted lying on the calendar or held out by someone whose face is in the frame, never in your hand.
- **Habit:** you ask to see the list first. **Quoted line:** "Dikhaiye." | "दिखाइए।" | "Let's see." Never "me" or "my" if the line goes on a button (rulebook Q8).
- **I know this person:** all six players (the review: being the grown-up child everyone phones works for every one of them).

## 4. Every pair, with the history that colours it

One line for each of the 55 pairs: the history, then the friction and the tenderness where they have both. Forms of address are the realism notes' proposals; readers confirm them.

**Nani with**
1. **Mummy:** Mummy learned to count change at Nani's counter at eight and swore never to run one; Nani lent her the shop's first stock money in 1999 and still tells her how to price fuses. Friction: advice nobody asked for. Tenderness: their phone call at nine every night.
2. **Papa:** the son-in-law from the railway colonies, who had no home town until he married in; Nani made him hers, teases his formality, and is the only one who lets him finish a sentence. He calls her "Mummy ji", books her ticket first, and she pours his tea first.
3. **Mama ji:** her youngest, who left at twenty and phones at eight every morning to worry. Friction: after the fall he wants her in his city flat ("My own bed," she says). Tenderness: she keeps his school cricket bat behind her door.
4. **Tinku:** Nani taught her to keep accounts on the grocery's old ledger; Tinku keeps Nani's carrom scores and medicine times, and is the only grandchild who drops every English word for her. Nani calls her "scorer", never "the youngest".
5. **Mausi:** her eldest, who married first and brings the food; Nani relies on Mummy for decisions and on Mausi for dinner, and Mausi minds. Tenderness: Mausi is the one Nani phones when she cannot sleep.
6. **Mausa ji:** the quiet son-in-law who has fixed her house's wiring for thirty years without being asked; she trusts him with her spare key. He calls her "Amma ji".
7. **Masterji:** when Mummy's shop opened next to his in 1999, Nani came to inspect the street and haggled with him for an hour over one hem; they have respected each other ever since, and he asks after her by name.
8. **Captain sahab:** met at Mummy's housewarming and argued cricket for an hour; each still believes they won.
9. **Mami:** the city daughter-in-law who logs the BP readings Nani speaks into her phone and sends the doctor a numbered list every month. Friction: whose kitchen it is during the wedding. Nani trusts her lists and pretends not to.
10. **You:** you have read her bills and letters aloud since you could read; you are her plain-truth person, the one she wants holding her list. She says तुम; you say "Nani" and आप.

**Mummy with**
11. **Papa:** married in 1997, and both say तुम to each other (the realism notes' equal-respect rule); her shutter faces his parcel office across the platform, and he waves when it goes up at nine. Friction: he times everything, she prices everything. Tenderness: when his phone dies on the night train, she reads him the time off her father's watch.
12. **Mama ji:** her younger brother, whom she half raised while Nani ran the counter; he calls her "Didi" and sends money she did not ask for. Friction: he solves things with money, she with time.
13. **Tinku:** Tinku works her Saturday counter and keeps the stock tally on her phone; Mummy is hardest on her, because Tinku is most like her.
14. **Mausi:** sisters three years apart: Mausi is the elder, Mummy the one Nani calls; rivalry dressed as concern, and a daily phone call they both look forward to.
15. **Mausa ji:** when she opened the shop, the lineman gave her her first wholesaler's name; his is the only advice on wiring she takes without arguing.
16. **Masterji:** shop neighbours sharing one wall for 27 years; he opens at ten and calls her nine "showing off". Every monsoon they lift each other's shutters.
17. **Captain sahab:** a lane neighbour who buys every fuse from her and haggles for sport; she sells him the floodlight bulbs for the lane cup at cost.
18. **Mami:** sisters-in-law who both run things; Mummy trusts her lists and doubts her city prices. At the wedding they split it, kitchen and guests, without a word.
19. **You:** she taught you to count change at the counter; in January she holds out the red pen ("Everyone calls you anyway"), her way of asking for help. She says तुम; you say आप, as to Papa.

**Papa with**
20. **Mama ji:** brothers-in-law with opposite clocks: Papa books in January, Mama ji books the night before and phones Papa to fix it. He calls Papa "Jija ji" and means "the family's railway".
21. **Tinku:** the only one who can make him laugh, by scoring his corrections ("That's four 'not 9:45's today"); he taught her to read a timetable, she taught him to read a scorebook.
22. **Mausi:** she brought his marriage proposal in 1996 and overrules his berth chart every trip; he books her a lower berth for her knee before she asks, and never says so.
23. **Mausa ji:** the two who married in sit side by side at every family function, contentedly silent; Papa talks trains, Mausa ji says "Hmm".
24. **Masterji:** Masterji stitched Papa's one good jacket and says his posture ruins it; Papa arrives at fittings to the minute, which Masterji finds suspicious.
25. **Captain sahab:** lane neighbours and two clocks: Captain sahab calls the cup for nine, Papa says 8:55. They walk at six every morning and never discuss it.
26. **Mami:** the other list-keeper: he sends her the guests' train times, she replies with a numbered list, and it is the happiest exchange of his year.
27. **You:** in May he hands you his list on the train, the first he has ever let go of; he texts "Reached?" and means "I missed you". He says तुम; you say "Ji Papa".

**Mama ji with**
28. **Tinku:** the uncle who brings gifts too expensive to use; she scores his promises ("You said March. Nil for one.").
29. **Mausi:** the eldest and the youngest: she half raised him too and still scolds him for the city; he pays for the benches in her tuition room.
30. **Mausa ji:** Mausa ji taught him to ride a scooter at fifteen; now Mama ji earns the most, and Mausa ji still pays for the tea first.
31. **Masterji:** Masterji measured him for his first interview shirt the summer Mummy's shop opened; now he orders his wedding kurta by phone and argues over photos of the cloth.
32. **Captain sahab:** little history; Captain sahab still remembers a catch Mama ji dropped in a family match years ago, and brings it up.
33. **Mami:** married in 2001 in the city; he promises, she plans; he says seventy, she counts sixty. They bicker in front of everyone and decide everything in private. She is the calm one, he is the worrier.
34. **You:** he calls you, not his sister, when something has to be done in Nani's town. "Sorted, Mama ji" is the sentence he most wants to hear.

**Tinku with**
35. **Mausi:** Tinku did her Class 3 sums in Mausi's tuition class; Mausi still checks them and tells everyone her marks.
36. **Mausa ji:** they watch matches side by side without talking: she scores, he says "Hmm" at every wicket. He is the only adult who never calls her "the youngest".
37. **Masterji:** he stitched her first school uniform and says she is too thin for any pattern; she brings his eleven o'clock tea on Saturdays.
38. **Captain sahab:** he taught her to keep the lane cup's book at twelve and now says the district should see it; she teases him about 1979. She is the successor he has not named.
39. **Mami:** Mami thinks Tinku should do a proper accounts course and says so at every function; Tinku keeps a tally.
40. **You:** her older sibling, and her rival for being the one everyone calls. She uses no address word for you, or "oye" and "sun" (never bhai or bro, which most players hear as a boy); you say तुम.

**Mausi with**
41. **Mausa ji:** married 34 years: she talks, he says "Hmm", she finishes his sentences and he lets her. On night trains they hold hands when they think the coach is asleep. Friction: she tells everyone his blood pressure.
42. **Masterji:** his customer for decades; she complains about every fitting and comes back every time.
43. **Captain sahab:** she once sent him a two-minute voice note about the lane cup's timings; he replied "9 am".
44. **Mami:** Mausi thinks city people waste money; Mami thinks Mausi feeds people too much. At the wedding Mausi's basket feeds Mami's list, and they are allies.

## 8. Clash check on the grids

- **No repeats** in silhouette hook, hair shape, height rank, primary colour, signature prop, voice tempo or typing style. Ten primary hues, ten props, ten hair shapes.
- **Near-misses, kept on purpose and flagged:**
  - **Spectacles on two people**, Nani (big round, always on) and Masterji (half-moon on the nose tip). They never share a chapter. This waits on the owner's spectacles question; the painter changes spectacle shapes between panels (the method notes' present-day sheet), so neither relies on them alone.
  - **Three paper props** (Papa's clear sleeve, Tinku's bound scorebook, Mami's hard clipboard). Each chapter's list of targets takes its asker's own form, so paper is the game's mechanic; the three are told apart by shape (floppy and clear, a thick block, a rigid board with a clip).
  - **Same value stack**: Papa and Masterji are light top over dark trousers; Captain sahab and Mami are dark top over light trousers. In each pair one has a pattern and the other is plain, the heights differ by 8 and 1 ranks, and they never share a chapter.
  - **Shared second colours**: black (Tinku, Masterji) and white (Captain sahab, Mami). Their keys still differ, since the first colour differs.
  - **Moustaches**: only Mama ji, unless the owner refuses Captain sahab's hat, when his fallback white moustache sits two chapters away from Mama ji's black one.
- **Two in one panel.** The pairs who most often share a panel have opposite top anchors: Papa (tall line, flat hair) with Mausi (wide round, puffed copper hair); Nani (seated triangle, white plait) with Mausa ji (square block, white brush-cut); Mummy (dark block, bun) with Masterji (light, bald, stooped); Tinku (seated angles, ponytail) with Captain sahab (standing side-on, hat); Mama ji (round, mid-stride) with Mami (tall line, upright). This guards against the painter swapping attributes between two people (method notes).
- **Rule 3 read.** Nothing above is a religious, caste or marital marker: no bindi, sindoor, mangalsutra, bangle, toe ring, head covering, thread, kara, turban, skullcap, safa or long beard. Two items need an Indian reader's yes: Mausi's big gold hoops (the realism notes call small plain hoops safe) and her copper-dyed hair. Mummy's watch must always be written as "a wristwatch with a round white face on a steel strap", or the painter may give her a plain steel band that reads as a kara.
