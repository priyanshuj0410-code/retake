# The First take: "Dadi's trunk"

Owner, 23 Oct 2026: "The story is very stupid... You are overfitting it to the questions and illustrations you already have. Keep the demo as is... Just draft a good, engaging narrative covering the five setups which we have identified and the questions we can ask in each of them. Don't focus on what we already have but on what will make this experience worth it and wow! for the players."

This replaces the "one monsoon night" story in [story.md](story.md). Nothing here is built.

## The idea in one line

Dadi is 78. She opens an old trunk, and five keepsakes pull out five days from her life, each from a different decade, each drawn in the popular art of its time. In each one, the player makes the calls that were made back then. That is the game's name: you **retake** her moments. At the end, her diary says what really happened.

## Why it can be a wow

1. **The game's name pays off.** "Retake" stops being a label. Each chapter is a scene from someone's life, played again with the player choosing.
2. **The five art styles finally mean something.** They are not five themes. They are five decades, each remembered in the media of its time:

   | Decade | Remembered as |
   |---|---|
   | 1971 | a railway poster |
   | 1977 | a pulp-novel cover |
   | 1984 | a travelling-show print |
   | 1992 | a sports-magazine spread |
   | 1999 | Doordarshan on the family TV |

   Every style change is time travel, so no cutscene has to explain why the world looks different.
3. **A trunk is something you can hold.** Each chapter opens with Dadi taking one thing out: a railway pass, a dog-eared pulp novel, a show ticket, a scorecard, a wedding card. The object fills the screen, and the art of its decade takes over. That is the whole transition, and it is literal: no metaphor to decode.
4. **A family across thirty years, in twenty minutes.** Asha, as Dadi was called before she was Dadi, is 23 in the first memory and 51 in the last. Her son is a boy keeping score in 1992 and the man asking her to sit down in 2026. Players watch a family grow while they play.
5. **Nostalgia for some, discovery for others.** Each memory is a recognisable Indian moment:
   - the first diesel engines arriving;
   - pulp novels at highway dhabas;
   - the travelling magic show at the winter fair;
   - the local cricket final;
   - the 1999 World Cup on Doordarshan, interrupted by the "rukavat" slide.

   Older players remember these. Younger players are in a world they have only heard about.
6. **The ending lands without preaching.** Nothing during play says the game is about bias. After the last decision, Dadi reads from her diary what really happened in each memory: who got the course, whose report was believed, who was kept home. That history then sits beside the player's own private reading. The point arrives as a story about real lives, not a lecture.

## The frame (present day, 2026)

- **Opening (about 45 seconds, five panels).**
  1. A family flat in Nashik on a rainy evening. Dadi's son is moving her into his home.
  2. In the store room is an old tin trunk, painted, with her initials.
  3. She opens it: a railway pass, a pulp novel, a ticket, a scorecard, a wedding card.
  4. She holds up the railway pass: "Some days I still replay."
  5. She hands the pass to the player: "This time, you decide."
- **Between chapters (about 15 seconds each).** Back in the flat, Dadi puts one thing down and picks up the next. One line from her, no more: "Six years later, I was married into a trucking family."
- **The finale (about 60 seconds, then the private reading).**
  1. Dadi takes out the last thing in the trunk, her diary.
  2. For each memory, one line of what really happened, read in her voice, on that decade's art.
  3. The private reading follows: the five result cards.
  4. Dadi closes the trunk and pushes it towards the player: "Now go and make your own days." This leads into the daily Next take.

## The five memories

Each memory is one event on one day. Each has one speaker, the person in charge back then, who voices the decisions. The cards are adults of that time and place, never Asha or her family. Each memory closes on a beat about the event, the same whoever was picked, and never on who got what.

### 1. 1971, Manmad junction: "The week the diesels came" (work)

- **Art:** the railway poster: enamel blue, black and cream.
- **Keepsake:** her first railway pass.
- **Asha's place:** 23, one of the first women clerks at the junction. The crew board and the duty roster pass through her hands.
- **The event:** the first diesel engines arrive. The steam crews must be trained, and the first diesel run to Bhusawal leaves on Friday.
- **Speaker:** the station master, a proud old steam man.
- **Decisions this setup can ask:**
  - Who trains on the new diesels (only two seats)?
  - Who drives the first diesel run, with the press on the platform?
  - Who is made senior driver when the old one retires?
  - Who gets the railway quarters next to the station?
  - Who is sent to the remote outpost for a year?
  - Who takes the day shift after a new baby, a mother or a father?
  - Whose complaint about the foreman goes up the line? (the hard topic)
- **Closing beat:** "Friday at dawn, the first diesel left Manmad on time."

### 2. 1977, the Kasara ghat: "Onion season" (safety and justice)

- **Art:** the pulp-novel cover: night violet, vermilion and marigold.
- **Keepsake:** a dog-eared pulp novel from a dhaba. Its cover is the first frame of the memory.
- **Asha's place:** 29, married into a family that runs four trucks on the ghat. She keeps the books at the depot at night.
- **The event:** the onion harvest has to go up the ghat in the monsoon, and there has been a run of highway robberies.
- **Speaker:** her father-in-law, who owns the trucks.
- **Decisions this setup can ask:**
  - Who drives the night convoy?
  - Who takes the cash run to the market?
  - Who is trained for the hazardous-goods licence?
  - Who is blamed for the scraped truck?
  - Who gets the bonus for the storm run?
  - Who sleeps at the depot, and who is sent home?
  - Whose report of being followed at night is taken up? (the hard topic)
- **Closing beat:** "That season, not one of our trucks was robbed."

### 3. 1984, the winter fair: "The magician's cash box" (trust)

- **Art:** the travelling-show print: indigo, vermilion and gold.
- **Keepsake:** a ticket stub for the Chandni Mandal.
- **Asha's place:** 36. Her cousin, a grown woman, has joined the travelling show as an assistant, and Asha comes to watch on the last night.
- **The event:** on the last night of the fair, the cash box is lighter than it should be, and the troupe leaves for the next town at dawn.
- **Speaker:** the magician, who owns the show.
- **Decisions this setup can ask:**
  - Whose account of the missing money do you believe?
  - Who keeps the key to the cash box?
  - Who closes the show, the star slot?
  - Who learns the secret of the big illusion?
  - Who is the face on the new poster?
  - Who goes to talk to the fair's officials?
  - Whose statement goes in the report about the man backstage? (the hard topic)
- **Closing beat:** "The money was found under the stage. The show went on."
- **Why it suits this memory:** a magic show is about where you look and whom you believe. It sits in the middle of the five, where the theme can glint without being named.

### 4. 1992, the old ground: "The final" (teams and leaders)

- **Art:** the sports-magazine centre spread: outfield greens and cherry red.
- **Keepsake:** a scorecard in her son's handwriting.
- **Asha's place:** 44. Her son, 14, is the club's scorer. She is in the stands with a flask of tea.
- **The event:** the town's tournament final against the mill team. The club picks its side, its order and its captain in one evening.
- **Speaker:** the coach.
- **Decisions this setup can ask:**
  - Who captains the final?
  - Who opens the batting?
  - Who is picked for the last three places?
  - Who is dropped after one bad game?
  - Who bowls the last over?
  - Who keeps wicket?
  - Who gets the club's one new kit?
  - Does a police complaint of violence at home cost a player the captaincy? (the hard topic)
- **Closing beat:** "The final went to the last ball. My son wrote every run."

### 5. 1999, the family flat: "The World Cup and a wedding" (home and family)

- **Art:** a 1990s family-magazine illustration and the Doordarshan screen, with its "rukavat ke liye khed hai" slide.
- **Keepsake:** her daughter's wedding card.
- **Asha's place:** 51, running the house. Her daughter's wedding is being fixed, and the World Cup is on TV.
- **The event:** a wedding week. The groom's family visits, the house is full, and everyone wants the TV for the match.
- **Speaker:** her mother-in-law, the eldest in the house.
- **Decisions this setup can ask:**
  - Sunday: who does what, and who gets the match?
  - Who gives up work to look after a parent who has fallen ill?
  - Who gets the room to study in?
  - Who keeps the household money and keys?
  - Whose job does the family move cities for?
  - Who goes to the talks with the groom's side, after a dowry demand? (hard)
  - Who should the children live with, when a cousin separates? (hard)
- **Closing beat:** "The wedding was fixed for December. India lost to Pakistan." *(This needs a fact-check before use.)*

## The finale: Dadi's diary

After the last decision, Dadi opens the diary. Each memory gets one line of what really happened, on that memory's art. History went against men too, so the diary includes both directions:

- **1971:** "Only men trained on the diesels. I stayed a clerk for eleven more years."
- **1971:** "A driver asked for time with his new son. They laughed him off the board."
- **1977:** "When a woman driver said a car followed her, my father-in-law said she imagined it. She left that winter."
- **1984:** "They believed the man's account. My cousin's was the same, word for word."
- **1992:** "The coach dropped a girl after one bad day. A boy after one bad day kept his place."
- **1999:** "My daughter's in-laws asked for a scooter. I said no. It cost us a year."

Then the five private cards: the player's own reading, with counts only and no verdict. Then the trunk passes to the player.

**Why the history comes only at the end:** if a chapter ended on "they chose the man", every chapter after it would turn into a test players try to pass. Chapters close on the event. The truth waits for the diary.

## What the cutscenes become

**Per chapter:** about four panels, all literal, all things that happen:
1. Dadi's hand takes the object from the trunk, drawn in the present-day style.
2. The object fills the frame and turns into that decade's style.
3. Two panels set the scene of the event, captioned by Asha.

**Words:** captions are at most 12 words, in Dadi's first person ("That year, the first diesels came to Manmad").

**Recurring faces:** Dadi in 2026 and Asha at five ages are the only faces that recur, and they never appear on a decision card. One portrait set of Asha at 23, 29, 36, 44, 51 and 78 needs painting, and checking that she reads as the same woman.

## Guardrails (what keeps the measurement clean)

1. The puzzles stay as designed: hidden equal pairs, twins, notes on the cards with decoys, the hard twin.
2. Asha and her family are never on a card. Each card person keeps one memory and one role, never a truck driver in one and a magician in another.
3. No outcome depends on a pick. Chapters close on the event, and the history arrives only after the last decision.
4. Loaded words come from each memory's speaker or a card note among decoys. The story never comments on a pick.
5. One hard topic per memory, two in 1999, each named plainly on the chapter card, with help lines checked before launch.
6. The content rules hold:
   - adults only;
   - no caste, religion or party;
   - the winter fair is not a religious festival;
   - no real political events (no 1975 to 1977 Emergency, no strikes);
   - every real date, such as the 1999 match, is fact-checked before it is used.
7. The narrative is a warm family story, not a story about bias. That keeps players from performing fairness during play.

## Two alternatives considered

- **"The magician's five acts."** Kader hosts the whole First take as a show in three parts: the pledge, the turn and the prestige. The reveal is the prestige: "every pair was equal; what moved you?" It is clever, and it mirrors the mechanic exactly. But it tells players from the start that a trick is being played, so they hunt for it and answer "fairly", the measurement problem the team just flagged. The magic show is kept as the middle memory instead.
- **"One town, one monsoon night"** (the current [story.md](story.md)). It is rejected (owner: "very stupid"): its links were fitted to existing paintings, and it had no person to care about.

### The opening, rewritten to introduce the family (about 60 seconds, 7 panels, present-day style)

1. A rainy evening in a Nashik flat, 2026. Boxes everywhere: Dadi is moving in with her son.
2. **Dadi**, 78: "Remembers everything. Forgives slowly."
3. **Vikram**, her son, 48, carrying the trunk up the stairs: "Keeps the peace. Mostly."
4. **Meera**, his wife, with the tea: "Hears everything first."
5. **Tara**, their daughter, 22, on the floor beside the trunk: "Asks what nobody asks." Then, to Dadi: "What's in this?"
6. The trunk opens on five keepsakes. Dadi: "Every one of these, someone had to choose."
7. She hands the player the railway pass: "Your turn. Let's see how you choose."

Between memories the present-day family chimes in, a line each, like a family listening to a story and arguing about it. Meera: "Wasn't that the year Mohan mama nearly quit?" Tara: "Dadi, you never told me that."

### How the family takes part (and the guardrails each needs)

1. **Introduced before used.** A family member always appears first in a cutscene, with name, face and one line, before they speak in a decision or appear on a card. The content checker enforces this.
2. **As the voice asking the question:** freely. The speaker is never a card in their own decision.
3. **As the gossip:**
   - Family members are where the notes come from: "Meera heard she can't take night runs", "Vikram says the coach thinks he's soft".
   - Gossip always touches several people on the cards, never only one of the matched pair. That makes the decoy remarks of change 6 natural, and the matched pair never stands out as the only person being talked about.
4. **As one of the options:** a guardrail is needed.
   - A known, liked face changes picks for reasons that have nothing to do with gender, which is the leak the twin design exists to stop.
   - So a family member can be a card only **outside the matched pair**, at most **once a chapter**. If the player picks them, that decision gives no reading. The cost is a few lost readings. In exchange, the family feels at stake: you can send your own brother on the run.
   - This is a change to the measurement design, so it goes to the research check after the usage reset, with the decoy notes.
5. **Each family member keeps one role per era,** and ages properly across eras: Mohan is a fireman in 1971 and a driver in 1977, never a magician.
6. **Every recurring face is painted at each age it appears,** and checked for likeness. Dadi needs ages 23, 29, 36, 44, 51 and 78; Vikram needs 14, 21 and 48.

## Does it scale? (owner, 23 Oct 2026: "is this scalable? because I would want to continue the same story in our dailies and event chapters")

It does not scale as Dadi's life alone:
- **Her life runs out.** The dailies need about 1,000 decisions a year, and one life cannot hold that many decisive days believably.
- **Each world is locked to one of her decades.** New worlds, such as an office, a clinic or a delivery app, have no place in her life.
- **The diary reveal is a one-time payoff.** Repeated daily, it becomes a formula.
- **A 78-year-old sole narrator** eventually forces her death into the story.

It scales as **the family's trunk.** The trunk holds keepsakes from every generation, and it passes to the player at the end of the First take.

| Layer | What it is | Narrator |
|---|---|---|
| The First take | The pilot: Dadi's five decades, as drafted above | Dadi |
| Weeks and dailies | Each week is one family member's stretch in one world and one era: six days of decisions, then Sunday's diary entry tells what really happened, beside the week's reading | Whoever the keepsake belongs to |
| Event chapters | One kind of event across generations, for example "Three weddings": Dadi's in 1970, her daughter's in 1999, her granddaughter's in 2026. The same decisions in three eras show what changed and what did not | One per era |

- **The content is endless.** The first five weeks walk Dadi's decades. The next cycle is her children's era, roughly 2000 to 2015, then her grandchildren's, 2015 to now.
- **New worlds arrive with new eras,** each in that era's popular art: a 2000s film-poster look for a call centre, a phone-screen look for a 2020s delivery app. "A decade looks like its media" keeps working.
- **The reveal becomes the weekly Sunday payoff.**
- **The narrators change.** Dadi's death never has to be a device. If it is ever used, it is a deliberate event chapter.

What it needs:
- **A family story bible:** the family tree, a timeline, places by era, and who is who. Every chapter is checked against it, and the content checker enforces that no family member is ever a decision card.
- **Portrait sets of the recurring family at several ages,** checked for likeness.
- **One art style for each new era.**

The measurement rules hold unchanged: the card people are the town's people of each era, no result depends on a pick, and history is revealed only after the decisions it covers.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Dadi as the narrator,** or should a grandfather tell some memories? A woman's life story leans the history towards women's barriers. The diary balances this by including men's, but the narrator's own story still leads.
2. **Real years and real events** (the 1999 World Cup, the diesel arrival), or "long ago" without dates? Real dates are more vivid but must be fact-checked.
3. **The five weeks after the First take:** does each week return to one of Dadi's decades, or move to present-day stories that the player now owns?
4. **The name "Asha":** is it the right name for young Dadi, or would you pick another?
