# One story through the First take and the first five weeks

Owner, 22 Oct 2026: the kite cutscene is "an ok placeholder, but we need a better continuous story stitching the puzzles and stories of the first take and the first five weeks". This draft is for review. It is built from the First take decisions in [first-take.md](first-take.md) (40 decisions, still a draft for the owner). The five weeks of dailies are not written yet: the arcs below are the frame for writing them. Cutscene mechanics and the first proof are in [cutscenes.md](cutscenes.md).

## The idea in one line

The five worlds are five places along the same stretch of road and rail, from the Kasara ghat up to Nashik, in one monsoon. A thing that leaves one world arrives in the next, so the player can follow it.

| World | The place | Who runs it (the speaker) |
|---|---|---|
| Station | the railway junction and its crew lobby: the night mail to Itarsi, goods to Manmad, the bridge over the Godavari at Nashik | Gurpreet, station master |
| Lugdi | the truck depot at the foot of the ghat: convoys up the hill, the onion runs | Mehul seth, who owns the trucks |
| Jadugar | the Chandni Mandal, a travelling magic show touring the towns along the highway | Kader, its magician |
| Cricket | the Purana Maidan in Nashik: the tin scoreboard under the neem tree | Shirin, the coach |
| Rukavat | Dadi's house in Nashik, where every day ends at 9 pm in front of the TV | Dadi |

The order is the First take's order, and it runs from the most public place to the most private: work, the road, the stage, the team, the home. The story makes that order feel like a journey instead of a list.

## Who carries the story

- **The player is an unnamed decider** (owner, 22 Oct 2026). They have no part in the story: the story happens around them, and each place's speaker asks them to decide.
- **The five speakers and things that move:** a train, a truck, a tent, a cup and a tiffin. They pass the story on.
- **Never a card person.** The people on the cards never carry the story or appear in a cutscene. If a face became a character, liking or disliking it would lean the picks.

## The rules the story keeps

1. **It never touches a decision.**
   - It does not hint at who matches whom.
   - It does not show what happened after a pick, and picks never change the story. Everyone gets the same story.
   - It says nothing about fairness. The words "test", "bias" and "attitude" never appear.
2. **Hard topics never appear in a cutscene.** They stay inside their own decisions, with their help lines.
3. **Every cutscene:**
   - is 4 to 6 panels and can be skipped;
   - opens in last week's style and ends in this week's;
   - joins the two on one torn-page seam panel, built as in the Lugdi to Station proof;
   - has captions as live text in all three languages, at most 12 words each.
4. **One monsoon is the clock.**
   - The First take is one rainy night.
   - The five weeks run from the first heavy rain to the river in flood, ending with the night mail leaving under a clear sky.

## The First take: one rainy night across the district

The First take already happens in one night and the Sunday after: the night mail, the night convoy, the late show, Sunday's captain and Dadi's "tonight". The story adds the road between the chapters.

**The story keeps moving** (owner, 22 Oct 2026): no painting is shared between the First take and the weeks. The First take's cutscenes tell its own night, and the week cutscenes go on from where it ended.

**The thread is the freight of the night,** not an object carried along (the kite was "a weak connection"):
- the onions stopped by mud on the line go up the ghat by road;
- the season's last truck carries the magic show;
- the show leaves its ground at dawn and the ground becomes Sunday's pitch;
- the match reaches Dadi's TV on the nine o'clock news.

**Captions say what happens and why, never a visual rhyme** (owner, 23 Oct 2026, of "Its headlights turn into spotlights": "wtf is this story?"). A seam panel shows something that literally happens in both styles.

**Six short cutscenes,** painted through `art/cutscenes.py` (prefix `ft`). Each seam is two paintings of one composition joined on a torn edge. The Hindi is a first draft for a native speaker. No caption names the Purana Maidan, because English only would turn it into "The Old Ground" mid-sentence.

How the puzzles sit in the night:
- **Station:** Gurpreet's crew lobby, from the night mail to the bridge in the rain.
- **Lugdi:** the depot, whose ghat road runs beside the rail line the player just left.
- **Jadugar:** the troupe that came up the same road.
- **Cricket:** the ground the troupe played on.
- **Rukavat:** the house that watched the match.

No decision is changed; only the ground between them.

## The five week-change cutscenes

Each has four panels: two in last week's world, the seam, and one in this week's world. A fifth or sixth panel is added only when the story needs it.

1. **Week 1 to 2, Station to Lugdi: "The line is closed".**
   1. The landslip on the rails, signals red, in enamel print.
   2. Gurpreet's lamp waving a goods train back.
   3. Seam: the ghat with the rail line on one side and the road on the other.
   4. Mehul seth's convoy taking the onion sacks up the road, in pulp gouache.

   This replaces the kite proof, which runs the other way (Lugdi to Station).
2. **Week 2 to 3, Lugdi to Jadugar: "The last load".**
   1. The depot at midnight.
   2. Painted trunks and a folded tent loaded onto a truck.
   3. Seam: the headlights on the road turning into a spotlight on a curtain.
   4. The tent rising in a small town, in show-poster style.
3. **Week 3 to 4, Jadugar to Cricket: "Pegs and stumps".**
   1. The last show's bow.
   2. The tent coming down at dawn.
   3. Seam: a tent peg pulled out of the ground and a stump driven in.
   4. The tin scoreboard under the neem tree, in magazine print.
4. **Week 4 to 5, Cricket to Rukavat: "On the nine o'clock news".**
   1. The cup on a plastic chair at the boundary.
   2. A press camera on the pavilion roof.
   3. Seam: the scoreboard turning into a TV screen.
   4. Dadi's front room, the family on the sofa, and the picture freezing with the interruption slide.
5. **Week 5 to the next month, Rukavat to Station: "The night mail".**
   1. The 9 pm film ends.
   2. A tiffin packed at the kitchen door.
   3. Seam: the TV's test card turning into the station's departure board.
   4. The night mail under a clear sky: the monsoon is over and the next month starts.

These week cutscenes go on from where the First take's night ended and are painted fresh, never reusing its panels. The kite is retired as a thread (owner: "a weak connection... we can do better").
