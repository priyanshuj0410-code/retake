## How every decision is built

1. **3 to 5 people, one question, pick then confirm.** Cards look the same before a choice and come in a new order for each player.
2. **Two of them have exactly the same numbers.** One is a woman, one a man. Nothing else on their cards differs except one thing, called the nudge here.
3. **The nudge** is the one thing that leans one way: the words the speaker uses about them, a voice pushing for or against one, a family detail, a hard fact said of one of them (a complaint of bullying they made, a report doubted, a police complaint against them, a wish to marry again, saying "no dowry"), or a detail that really matters to the job (a section learned, a licence held, who saw it, dust that makes one ill, who keeps wicket).
4. **Every decision has a twin**, four decisions later in the same chapter, with a different two people and the nudge on the other side: on the man if it was on the woman, and the other way round. The two halves of a twin share no person, so liking or disliking one face cannot read as a lean. Both halves sit in the same chapter, so a chapter is complete on its own.
5. **How a twin reads.**
   - The woman both times: a lean towards women.
   - The man both times: a lean towards men.
   - Following the nudge both times: no lean from this twin.
   - Picking outside the matching two: no reading from that decision.
   - "Towards the men" always means the men got the seat, the run, the trust, the benefit of the doubt or the lighter share. Both directions count exactly the same.
6. **Nudges that matter.** One twin in each chapter has a nudge that really matters. There, following it both times is the player's own sound rule, and simply giving the woman (or the man) the same answer both times no longer looks steady. This is what stops "always the same answer" from passing.
7. **Hard twins.** Each chapter also has one hard twin (Rukavat has two), never the twin whose nudge matters: it carries the chapter's hard topic. It reads like any other twin: what counts is whether the hard fact weighs the same on a woman as on a man. Holding it against both, or against neither, is no lean from that twin; holding it against one side only is a lean. Only the speaker ever says a slanted thing about it, and the hard fact is never said of a name chosen to mark a community. Suggested: result cards 2 and 3 never draw on a hard twin, so no one is told "you picked the man both times" about a police complaint.
8. **The Kind column.** Each chapter's twin table names every twin's kind: "matters" for the twin whose nudge really matters, "hard" for the hard topic, and "plain" for the other two. So every chapter has one matters twin, one hard twin and two plain twins, except Rukavat, which has two hard twins and one plain.
9. **The lean figure itself is the research line's.** This draft only fixes what each decision can show. How the 20 twins become one number, and how sure it is, waits on the research line.

**A note on the cast.** With the fourteen new people in the First take, no two people are tied twice anywhere in the 40 decisions: 38 different pairs, and the only repeats are each batting order's two stages (Cricket 1 and 2, Cricket 5 and 6), which share their pair by design. Every pair is one woman and one man, and no speaker is in a pair of their own world. Pairs must look alike in age on their portraits: inside every pair the portrait ages are within 8 years, except Jasleen and Dinesh in Lugdi 1 and Shampa and Mahesh in Rukavat 4 (10 years each), whom the art check looks at first. Pairs with a new person are confirmed once that portrait is painted. The full pair plan, and the check that confirms it, is in [cast.md](cast.md). New people also sit on cards outside the matching two, at least two such cards each in their chapter. In every world a new person is in the matching two no more often than the regulars are, as a share of the cards they appear on. In Jadugar and Cricket that holds since the second round added Dolly and Lalit, and Zarina and Uday (O17, answer 3); before it, Jonali, Thomas, Leena and Chandan were in the matching two on about 4 of their 6 cards. In Jadugar and Cricket every matching two still holds one new face, because every pair of matching age among the regulars is already used in another chapter and no pair is used twice; the checker prints, for each chapter, how many matching two have no new face (Station 2, Lugdi 3, Jadugar 0, Cricket 0, Rukavat 3). A Next take, with more pairs to draw on, mixes them. In Jadugar, Thomas was in the matching two of Jadugar 1 and 2, two decisions running; the two "send a" decisions now swap places, so no one there is.

## The Next take: a fresh set each month

1. **One set a month.** Each month has its own set of 40 decisions, five chapters in the same order, written, reviewed by you and in Hindi before the month starts, and kept four weeks ahead like the dailies.
2. **Nothing comes back.** No situation, nudge wording or set of numbers is used twice, ever, in the starter or the dailies. A ledger lists every decision used; the build check refuses a set that repeats one.
3. **The same two people.** Two people tied in a world one month are not tied in that world the next month. You chose more people for this on 22 Oct 2026 (O17, answer 3): with the twenty new people and the age rule (portraits within 8 years), each world has 17 to 19 woman-and-man pairs of matching age: Station 18, Lugdi 17, Jadugar 19, Cricket 18, Rukavat 19 (counted in [cast.md](cast.md)). 4. **The phone remembers which sets it has played,** next to the reading and never sent. A Next take always uses the newest set this phone has not played.
5. **The Next take opens on the 1st of the month after the last reading, and never sooner than 28 days on.** You chose this rule, rule B, on 22 Oct 2026. Sets are made one per calendar month, each ready on the 1st. A Next take always uses the next month's set, which is ready by then, so nothing waits for a set, and takes stay 28 to 31 days apart. The Week tab shows the date it opens, never a countdown.
   - **Worked examples.** Finish on 1 March: it opens on 1 April. Finish on 15 March: 1 April is too soon, so it opens on 12 April.
   - **The only wait** is a month's set being late, which writing four weeks ahead (point 1) is there to stop. If it ever happens, the row shows the day that set comes out.

6. **A begun take keeps its set** until it is finished, even into a new month.
7. **Each month changes:** the situations; who is paired with whom; every number (still exactly matched inside each two); which half of each twin carries the nudge on the woman first; and which nudges are used, so each area's ideas come back in new places.
8. **A Next take must still work for someone who knows how the first was made.** From the second set on, each chapter has two twins with a nudge that matters instead of one; at least three decisions a chapter carry a second tie between two women or two men, so a tie does not always mean a woman and a man; and the matching two are not always the best on the numbers: sometimes tied for the last place or the middle of a rank.
9. **The starter's decisions never appear as dailies,** and a starter's situation is not reused in a daily for a year.
