Before you start

How to use this workbook. Who fills what, and the rules.

The form has five sections, covered here in the same order. Every question is a card. The chip on each card says what it needs:

Readythe answer is settled: copy it in. Facts neededonly the team knows it: fill it in. Writea written answer, in your own words, from the material on the card. Team leadthe team lead does this personally.

Write in your own words

Solve says: "We do not recommend using generative AI tools to write your application for you", because such answers read as generic and "can strongly disadvantage your application." Use the material on each card as notes, and write the answer yourself. AI tools may then be used, as Solve suggests, to shorten a draft or check the grammar.

Women in India are about half as likely as men to be in paid work. One reason is the everyday decisions made about them at work: who is hired, sent, trusted and promoted. Retake trains and measures those decisions, and Solve's support would take it into workplaces and prove it.

That is the one idea every answer comes back to. Keep the answers consistent with it.

Four claims we must not make

The form's Accuracy and Integrity Agreement says that exaggerating "may lead to the removal of my solution from the Challenge." So, in every answer:

  • No users yet. No workplace has used Retake. The prototype has been played by the team and in walkthroughs only.
  • No paying customers yet. Unless the team has a signed partner, the business model is a plan.
  • The reading is not validated. How many decisions a confident reading needs, and how they become one figure, is still being worked out.
  • Retake shows choices inside the game. We do not claim it changes who gets hired or promoted. That is what a pilot would test.

11What specific problem are you solving?Required200 wordsWrite
Solve asksThe specific problem within the Challenge; its scale where we work and globally; how many people it affects; what causes it.
Material
  • The gap. Solve's own Challenge page: "women remain 24% less likely than men to participate in the labor force." In India, 41.7% of women aged 15 and over are in the labour force, against 78.8% of men (Periodic Labour Force Survey 2023-24, MoSPI).
  • Part of it is decided inside workplaces. Who is hired, given the shift or the route, sent on the course, promoted, believed. When science faculty rated identical applications from "John" and "Jennifer", John was judged more competent and hireable and offered a higher salary (Moss-Racusin and colleagues, 2012).
  • The bias is common. UNDP's 2023 Gender Social Norms Index finds nearly 9 in 10 people worldwide hold at least one bias against women. Found in secondary reports: confirm in the UNDP report before citing.
  • Training rarely reaches the decision. Gender training at work is usually a one-off session counted by attendance, so no one can see whether decisions get fairer. One-off diversity training has weak and short-lived effects (Dobbin and Kalev, 2016). Team: add an example from a company you know, if you have one.

12What is your solution?Required200 wordsWrite
Solve asksWhat it is, what it does, how it works and what technology it uses, in simple terms. About the solution, not the problem. A demo link if relevant.
Material
  • A daily puzzle app set in five illustrated Indian workplaces and homes: a railway crew lobby, a truck depot, a travelling show, a cricket club and a family home. Three short decisions a day.
  • Each decision is a real kind of workplace call: who drives the night train, who becomes senior loco pilot, who carries the cash, who captains, who gets the course seat. It shows 3 to 5 people with their records.
  • Two of them have identical records, one woman and one man. One detail differs, such as the boss calling her "pushy" and him "takes charge". Later, a twin decision puts that detail on the other side, with different people.
  • The reading comes from the pair. The man both times is a lean towards men; the woman both times is a lean towards women; following the detail both times is no lean. In some twins the detail really matters, such as who holds the licence.
  • What each player gets: a private reading, a "look again" at the pair, and practice on new cases. Leaderboards reward fair play both ways.
  • What runs today: a 20-minute starter of five chapters, in Hinglish, Hindi and English, installable on a phone and working offline. Planned, not built: an anonymous summary for employers showing which kinds of decision lean.

Section 5 · More About Your Solution and Business Model

Why it is new, why it will work, and how it pays for itself.

31What makes your solution innovative?300 wordsWrite
Solve asksHow it approaches the problem in a new or much improved way; how it differs from others; how it could change the field.
Material
  • It works on the decision, not on attitudes. Most gender training is a one-off talk, and its effects are weak and fade (Dobbin and Kalev, 2016). Implicit-bias tests have a weak link to what people do (Forscher and colleagues, 2019).
  • It measures decisions, using the logic of the identical-CV studies, inside a game people choose to play.
  • It reads a lean either way, towards men or towards women, and separates being swayed by a word from being swayed by gender. No one is accused, and favouring women counts as much as favouring men.
  • A daily habit, not a workshop: three short decisions a day, with leaderboards that reward fair play.
  • Made for Indian workplaces, including frontline ones: Indian settings, names and languages, from Hinglish to Hindi, on a phone, offline.
  • For the field: a way for organisations to see whether their gender training changes decisions, which attendance counts cannot show.

32Describe in simple terms how and why you expect your solution to have an impact on the problem.300 wordsWrite
Solve asksOur theory of change: how activities lead to outputs and to short- and long-term outcomes, with evidence for each link (research, interviews, our own data).
Material
  • Activities: managers and employees play three short decisions a day; each gets a private reading and a "look again" at the pair they treated differently; the organisation sees anonymous totals.
  • Outputs: a private reading per person; a map of which kinds of decision lean, such as promotions as against shift assignments.
  • Short-term outcomes: decision-makers notice when a word or detail is doing the work; HR adds clear criteria where the organisation leans.
  • Long-term outcomes: fairer hiring, assignment and promotion; more women entering work and moving up.
  • Evidence for the links: identical-application studies show bias acts in decisions (Moss-Racusin 2012); clear criteria and structured decisions reduce bias (Bohnet, What Works, 2016); noticing and practice can reduce bias (Devine and colleagues, 2012).
  • The honest gap: whether a change inside the game carries into real decisions is what the pilot will measure, against a partner's own records.

33Describe your solution's tech stack and how you know that it works.300 wordsWrite
Solve asksThe technology, and proof that it works. For new technology, a paper or other public evidence.
Material
  • The app: a web app that installs on a phone and works offline (a Progressive Web App), built in React and TypeScript. The puzzles are data, so new content needs no new code. Three languages switch on every screen.
  • The reading: every decision has a twin built to the same rules. An automated check refuses any set where the pair's numbers differ, where a person repeats, or where a hard fact is said of a name that marks a community.
  • The art: illustrations are made with an open image model run on our own computer, then reviewed and corrected by hand. Players never interact with AI, and AI plays no part in the reading.
  • How we know it works: the playable demo (the video shows it), automated accessibility and layout checks, and written content rules that every puzzle must pass.
  • Not yet built or proven: the anonymous employer summary, and that the reading is a valid measure. Say so, and that both come first in the funded work.
