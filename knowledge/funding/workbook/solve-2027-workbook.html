Before you start

How to use this workbook. Who fills what, and the rules.

The form has five sections, covered here in the same order. Every question is a card. The chip on each card says what it needs:

Readythe answer is settled: copy it in. Facts neededonly the team knows it: fill it in. Writea written answer, in your own words, from the material on the card. Team leadthe team lead does this personally.

Write in your own words

Solve says: "We do not recommend using generative AI tools to write your application for you", because such answers read as generic and "can strongly disadvantage your application." Use the material on each card as notes, and write the answer yourself. AI tools may then be used, as Solve suggests, to shorten a draft or check the grammar.

Gender and relationships education is judged by asking students what they think. Retake measures what they do. It works now with adults, and Solve's support would validate it and build the version for students.

That is the one idea every answer comes back to. Keep the answers consistent with it.

Four claims we must not make

The form's Accuracy and Integrity Agreement says that exaggerating "may lead to the removal of my solution from the Challenge." So, in every answer:

  • No users yet. The prototype has been played by the team and in walkthroughs only.
  • The student version does not exist. What exists and works is the assessment with adult content.
  • The reading is not validated. How many decisions a confident reading needs, and how they become one figure, is still being worked out.
  • Retake shows choices inside the game. It does not claim a change in behaviour outside it.

Good to know

  • Public answers. The Solution Profile becomes public on Solve's site if we reach the semi-finals.
  • The team lead travels. If we are selected as Finalists, the team lead pitches and interviews with judges over Zoom in February. If we are selected as Solvers, the team lead must attend Solver Orientation in Cambridge, MA in April 2027 (Solve gives travel assistance) and the Solve Challenge Finals in New York in September. Start on the US visa early.
  • Saving. The form saves as you go, and you can skip between sections. Nothing counts until someone clicks Submit Solution.
  • English only. Every answer and uploaded file must be in English.

11What specific problem are you solving?Required200 wordsWrite
Solve asksThe specific problem within the Challenge; its scale where we work and globally; how many people it affects; what causes it.
Material
  • Bias is near universal. UNDP's 2023 Gender Social Norms Index: nearly 9 in 10 people worldwide hold at least one bias against women. For India the index reports 99.2%, from 2010 to 2014 survey data. Both found in secondary reports: confirm them in the UNDP report before citing.
  • It shows in decisions, not in what people say. When science faculty rated identical applications from "John" and "Jennifer", John was judged more competent and hireable and offered a higher salary (Moss-Racusin and colleagues, 2012).
  • The gap is in assessment. Gender and relationships education, including our own programmes, is judged by surveys before and after: what learners say they believe. Students soon learn the right answer to give. Teachers cannot see whether a learner can make a fair decision, or where a class still leans, so they have nothing to act on.
  • Our scale: how many students our school programmes reach, and where. Team: add the real numbers.
Draft
12What is your solution?Required200 wordsWrite
Solve asksWhat it is, what it does, how it works and what technology it uses, in simple terms. About the solution, not the problem. A demo link if relevant.
Material
  • A daily puzzle app set in five illustrated Indian worlds: a railway station, a truck depot, a travelling magic show, a cricket club and a family home.
  • Each decision shows 3 to 5 people with their records and asks one question: who drives the night train, who captains, who does the chores.
  • Two of them have identical records, one woman and one man. One detail differs, such as a word the speaker uses about one of them. Later, a twin decision puts that detail on the other side, with different people.
  • The reading comes from the pair. The man both times is a lean towards men; the woman both times is a lean towards women; following the detail both times is no lean. In some twins the detail really matters, such as who holds the licence, so "always pick the woman" does not look fair.
  • Players never see the words bias or test and never know which two match. They get a private reading and a "look again" at the pair.
  • What runs today: a 20-minute starter of five chapters, in Hinglish, Hindi and English, installable on a phone and working offline.

Section 5 · More About Your Solution and Business Model

Why it is new, why it will work, and how it pays for itself.

31What makes your solution innovative?300 wordsWrite
Solve asksHow it approaches the problem in a new or much improved way; how it differs from others; how it could change the field.
Material
  • Surveys can be answered to please. Implicit tests, which time how fast people react, have a weak link to what people do: a 2019 review by Forscher and colleagues found that changing those scores did not change behaviour.
  • Retake measures the decision itself, using the logic of the identical-CV studies, inside a game.
  • The paired design reads a lean either way, towards men or towards women, and separates being swayed by a word from being swayed by gender.
  • A daily habit, so the reading repeats over time: an assessment a teacher can use as it goes, not a one-off test.
  • Made for India: Indian places, names and languages, from Hinglish to Hindi.
  • For the field: a way to evaluate gender and relationships education by what learners do, which programmes like ours have never had.

32Describe in simple terms how and why you expect your solution to have an impact on the problem.300 wordsWrite
Solve asksOur theory of change: how activities lead to outputs and to short- and long-term outcomes, with evidence for each link (research, interviews, our own data).
Material
  • Activities: learners play short sets of paired decisions in class or at home; each gets a private reading and a "look again" at the pair they treated differently; the facilitator sees the class pattern.
  • Outputs: a reading per learner; a class map of which situations sway decisions.
  • Short-term outcomes: learners notice when a word or detail is doing the work; facilitators target the sessions where a class leans.
  • Long-term outcomes: fairer decisions on cases learners have never practised, which is the gain the assessment can show; fairer choices in school and later at work.
  • Evidence for the links: identical-application studies show that bias acts in decisions (Moss-Racusin 2012); a study of breaking prejudiced habits showed that noticing and practice can reduce bias (Devine and colleagues, 2012). Team: add anything from our own sessions.
  • The honest gap: whether a change inside the game carries into real life is what the pilot will test.
Draft
33Describe your solution's tech stack and how you know that it works.300 wordsWrite
Solve asksThe technology, and proof that it works. For new technology, a paper or other public evidence.
Material
  • The app: a web app that installs on a phone and works offline (a Progressive Web App), built in React and TypeScript. The puzzles are data, so new content needs no new code. Three languages switch on every screen.
  • The reading: every decision has a twin built to the same rules. An automated check refuses any set where the pair's numbers differ, where a person repeats, or where a hard fact is said of a name that marks a community.
  • The art: illustrations are made with an open image model run on our own computer, then reviewed and corrected by hand. Learners never interact with AI, and AI plays no part in the reading.
  • How we know it works: the playable demo (the video shows it), automated accessibility and layout checks, and written content rules that every puzzle must pass.
  • Not yet proven: that the reading is a valid measure. Say so, and that it is the first thing the funding pays for.
