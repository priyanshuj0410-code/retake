12What is your solution?Required200 wordsWrite
Solve asksWhat it is, what it does, how it works and what technology it uses, in simple terms. About the solution, not the problem. A link to a video of a product demo, if relevant.
Material
  • A phone game of fair decisions set in five illustrated Indian worlds: a railway junction, trucks on a ghat road, a travelling magic show, a cricket ground and a family home.
  • Each decision shows 3 to 5 people with their records and asks one question: who drives the ghat section tonight, whose story do you believe, who captains the first XI, who does what on Sunday.
  • Two of them have identical records, one woman and one man. One detail differs, such as a word on one person's card or a worry about their family. Later, a twin decision puts that detail on the other side, with a different equal pair.
  • The reading comes from the pair. The man both times is a lean towards men; the woman both times is a lean towards women; following the detail both times is no lean. In some twins the detail really matters, such as who holds the licence, so "always pick the woman" does not look fair.
  • Players never see the words bias, test or gender, and the pair is never marked. The aim is that they cannot tell which two match. Still to fix, for the research check: the notes that pass on gossip ("..., Mohan says") appear on 23 of the 83 other people's cards and on none of the hidden pair's 80, so they can point at the pair.
  • At the end, a private reading of five cards, worked out on the phone: how often they picked the woman or the man when the two had the same numbers; one twin side by side; the part of life that leaned most; a tip; and a last card saying it is a first reading from one sitting. Hard-topic twins are not used for the twin and part-of-life cards.
  • What runs today: the First take, live at https://tel-retake.vercel.app: 5 chapters, 40 decisions of eight kinds, 13 painted cutscenes and the reading; in Hinglish, Hindi (a draft) and English; installs on a phone and works offline; asks for a birth date first and keeps out anyone under 18; nothing leaves the phone. Each chapter card says "About 4 minutes", so about 20 in all: an estimate, never timed. Link the pitch video from question 10 here. The app link goes in question 20.
  • Planned, not built: three dailies a day (Easy, Medium and Hard), a streak, leaderboards and accounts. If you mention them, say they are planned.
