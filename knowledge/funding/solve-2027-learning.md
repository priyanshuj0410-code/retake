- Retake shows choices inside the game. It does not claim a change in behaviour outside it.

### The problem (material)

- **Gender bias is near universal and starts early.**
  - UNDP's 2023 Gender Social Norms Index finds nearly 9 in 10 people worldwide hold at least one bias against women.
  - For India, the index reports 99.2% (from 2010 to 2014 survey data).
  - Both figures are taken from secondary reports. Confirm them in the UNDP report before citing.
- **The bias shows in decisions, not in what people say.** Equal records are judged unequally once a name signals gender. Moss-Racusin and colleagues (2012, PNAS) sent science faculty identical applications from "John" and "Jennifer": John was rated more competent and hireable and offered a higher salary.
- **The learning gap is in assessment.** Gender and relationships education, including The Equal Lens's own programmes, is evaluated with self-report surveys before and after. These measure what learners say they believe. Students quickly learn the right answer to give. So schools and programmes cannot see whether a learner can actually make a fair decision, or where a class still leans, and teachers have nothing to act on.
- **Scale [you]:** how many students The Equal Lens's school programmes reach, or plan to reach, and in which states.

### The solution (material)

- **What a decision looks like:** Retake is a daily puzzle app with five illustrated Indian worlds (a railway station, a truck depot, a travelling magic show, a cricket club and a family home). Each decision shows 3 to 5 people with their records and asks one question: who drives the night train, who captains, who does the chores.
- **How it reads a lean:**
  - Two of the people have identical records, one a woman and one a man. A single detail, such as a word the speaker uses about one of them, differs.
  - Later comes a twin decision with different people and that detail on the other side.
  - The reading comes from the pair. Choosing the man both times is a lean towards men, choosing the woman both times is a lean towards women, and following the detail both times is no lean.
  - Some twins have a detail that really matters, such as who holds the licence. This stops "always pick the woman" from looking fair.
- **What players see:** players never see the words bias or test, and never know which two match. They get a private reading and a "look again" at the equal pair.
- **What runs today:** a 20-minute starter called the First take (5 chapters), plus the daily puzzles. It runs in Hinglish, Hindi and English and installs as an app that works offline.
- **Demo link:** **[you]** decide whether to share the demo link. It is "anyone with the link" today.

### Who it serves (material)

- **Now:** adults, because the assessment is being proven with adults first. Testing an unvalidated measure on children would be wrong, and adults can consent to hard topics and to the use of their data.
- **Next, the target of this application:**
  - students aged 13 to 18 in Indian schools, through The Equal Lens's Teen Compass (13 to 15) and Relay (16 to 18) programmes, and the teachers and facilitators who run them;
  - learners in Hindi-medium and mixed-language classrooms, who are poorly served by English-only tools.
- **How it meets their needs:**
  - each learner gets a private reading and practice on unfamiliar cases;
  - the facilitator sees where a class leans and which situations sway it, so the next session targets that.
- **Child safety, which the challenge requires for tools serving children:**
  - the adult hard topics stay out;
  - no public leaderboards for minors;
  - each student's reading stays private;
  - parental consent follows India's rules for children's data.

### Innovative (material)

- Most measures of gender attitudes are surveys, which learners can answer to please. Implicit tests (reaction-time measures) have weak links to what people do: a 2019 meta-analysis by Forscher and colleagues found that changing implicit scores did not change behaviour.
- Retake measures the decision itself, using the logic of the classic identical-CV studies, built into a game.
- The paired design reads a lean in either direction, towards men or towards women, and it separates being swayed by a word from being swayed by gender.
- The daily format brings learners back, so the reading is repeated over time. That makes it formative assessment, not a one-off test.
- It is set in Indian places, names and languages, from Hinglish to Hindi.

### Theory of change (material)

- **Activities:**
  - learners play short sets of paired decisions in class or at home;
  - each gets a private reading and a "look again" at the pair they treated differently;
  - the facilitator sees the class pattern.
- **Outputs:** a reading per learner; a class map of which situations sway decisions.
- **Short-term outcomes:**
  - learners notice when a word or detail is doing the work;
  - facilitators target sessions where the class leans.
- **Long-term outcomes:** fairer decisions on cases learners have never practised, which is the gain the assessment can show; fairer choices in school and later at work.
- **Evidence for the links:**
  - identical-application studies show that decisions are where bias acts (Moss-Racusin 2012);
  - research on breaking prejudiced habits shows that noticing and practice can reduce bias (Devine and colleagues, 2012);
  - **[you]**: anything from your own sessions.
- **The honest gap:** the link from in-game change to real-world change is what the pilot will test.

### Tech stack and how you know it works (material)

- **App:**
  - a web app that installs on a phone and works offline (a Progressive Web App);
  - React and TypeScript;
  - the puzzles are data, so new content needs no new code;
  - three languages switch on every screen.
- **Reading logic:** every decision has a twin with the same matched pair design. An automated content check refuses any set where the pair's numbers differ, a person repeats, or a hard topic is said of a name that marks a community.
- **Proof it works today:**
  - the playable demo;
  - automated accessibility and layout checks;
  - written content rules.
- **Not yet proven:** the validity of the reading. Say that it is the first use of the funding.
- **Art:** illustrations are made with an open image model running locally, then reviewed and corrected by hand.

### Business model and financial sustainability (material)

- **Free for learners and schools.** The Equal Lens's programmes are free to delivery partners.
- **Who pays:**
  - grants and corporate CSR funds, since India's Companies Act requires large companies to spend 2% of profits on social causes and education is an eligible area;
  - later, state education departments.
- **Cross-subsidy:** the adult workplace version (gender-fair decision training for companies) can be sold to fund the school version.
- **Evidence to date [you]:** grants received, partner schools, CSR conversations. If there are none yet, say what is in progress, honestly.
