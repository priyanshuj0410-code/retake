# Everyday life moments for a fresh First take

The owner's problem (25 Oct 2026): the current First take uses unfamiliar settings from several decades. The fix asked for: moments everyone goes through, like the shaadi demo.

**How to read this**
- Each moment gives: who goes through it (with a number where one was found), the decisions about people inside it, the hard topic that fits it, what varies and how to write it so most players recognise it, and the risk that it reads as one community's custom (Low, Medium, High).
- The decision kinds are the game's own: Pick one, Pick a team, Rank, Batting order, Split, Believe, Spend checks. "Twin-friendly" marks a moment where a woman and a man in the same position come up naturally, so a swapped half reads as ordinary.
- Every number has a source in the list at the end, marked [S1] and so on. "Primary" means the official report or the organisation's own page was opened. "Secondary" means a news or exam-prep page reporting it was opened, not the report itself. A few figures were seen only in search summaries; they are marked "summary only" and need checking before use.
- Figures are given with their table or indicator so they can be checked; word-for-word quoting is kept short.

## Home and family

### M01. A family wedding week
- **Who goes through it:** nearly every adult, as family or guest. A trade body, CAIT, estimated about 48 lakh weddings in November and December 2024 alone, with spending bands from about Rs 3 lakh to over Rs 1 crore a wedding [S12] (an estimate from traders, not a count). In IHDS 2011-12, 95% of women said their families were involved in choosing their husband [S15].
- **Decisions about people:** who handles the cash and the caterer (Pick one); who drives which car (Rank or Pick a team); the welcome-desk team (Pick a team); the night-before jobs (Split); who is believed when the gift envelope goes missing (Believe). Twin-friendly: two cousins, a woman and a man, both offering to handle the money.
- **Hard topic inside:** a dowry demand from the other side. In a rural survey (REDS 2006), dowry was paid in 95% of marriages from 1960 to 2008, and the World Bank authors find it in all major religious groups [S13]. In 2022, 13,479 cases were registered under the Dowry Prohibition Act and 6,450 dowry deaths [S14, secondary]. Also: a guest who bothers someone at the function (harassment).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** rituals (pheras, nikah, a church service, Anand Karaj, a morning muhurtam in the South), how many days, what is spent. Write the logistics every wedding shares: the guest list, food for hundreds, a vendor who lets you down, cash, transport, the hall, an elder who falls ill. Say "the wedding", keep rituals off screen.
- **Risk:** Medium. "Baraat", "sangeet", "haldi" and dress read North Indian, often Hindu or Punjabi. Low if every crisis is a logistics crisis.
- **World fit:** Lugdi (the chaos as a pulp caper) or Jadugar (the stage night). Area: home and family; teams and leaders.

### M02. Moving into a new home after marriage
- **Who goes through it:** most women. In PLFS 2020-21, 47.9% of women were migrants against 10.7% of men, and marriage was the reason for 86.8% of women migrants (6.2% of men) [S3, Statement 1 and the reasons table].
- **Decisions about people:** whose job decides the city when a couple has offers in two cities (Pick one; twin-friendly by design); who in the new house shows the newcomer the ropes; which jobs the newcomer gets (Split); whose name goes on the rent agreement.
- **Hard topic inside:** giving up paid work and the care burden; dowry harassment after marriage (as a situation only).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** some communities in Meghalaya and Kerala do not follow the bride-moves pattern; urban couples move into a rented flat instead of a joint house. Write "moving after the wedding" so both a joint house and a flat fit.
- **Risk:** Low to Medium. Moving in with the husband's family spans religions; the "new bahu" framing can feel like a TV serial.
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: home and family; work.

### M03. The big festival at home
- **Who goes through it:** nearly everyone. Pew's 2019-20 India survey: Diwali is celebrated by 95% of Hindus, 98% of Jains, 90% of Sikhs, 79% of Buddhists, 31% of Christians and 20% of Muslims; 17% of Hindus join Christmas and 7% Eid [S5].
- **Decisions about people:** the festival jobs (Split: cleaning, cooking, shopping, lights, guests); whose parents the couple visits this year (Pick one; twin-friendly); who hosts; who holds the gift money.
- **Hard topic inside:** the care burden. Time Use Survey 2024: 81.5% of women and 27.1% of men (age 6 and over) did unpaid domestic work on the survey day, for 289 and 88 minutes a day per person who did it [S2, Tables 1 and 2]. Also safety: a relative who drinks, a fireworks injury.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** which festival, its food and rituals. Write the shared shape: a full house, a big meal, cleaning, travel, gifts. In the First take keep it "festival week" with no name; in the dailies, name festivals and rotate them through the year (Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Onam, Pongal, Durga Puja, Lohri, Bihu).
- **Risk:** High if one festival frames the First take (Diwali reads Hindu and North Indian). Low if festival-neutral or rotated.
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: home and family.

### M04. Relatives come to stay
- **Who goes through it:** no national number found; near-universal by reasoning, since guests come for weddings, illness, exams and festivals.
- **Decisions about people:** who gives up their room; who fetches them from the station at 5 am; who cooks; who takes the guest to the hospital or the market (Split, Pick one).
- **Hard topic inside:** the care burden [S2]; a relative who makes a young person uncomfortable (harassment inside the family; a situation, never shown).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** house size (one room, a flat, a village house). Write the squeeze, not the house.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Rukavat or Station (the pick-up). Area: home and family; safety.

### M05. A new baby in the family
- **Who goes through it:** nearly every family, often. Registered births were 254.4 lakh in 2022 [S7]. NFHS-5: 88.6% of births are in a hospital or health centre [S1, indicator 50].
- **Decisions about people:** who takes leave when a new mother and a new father in the same office both ask for the same week (Pick one; twin-friendly); the night feeds (Split); who stays at the hospital; whose career pauses.
- **Hard topic inside:** son preference: 94% of Indians say it is very important to have at least one son, 90% a daughter [S4]; NFHS-5 sex ratio at birth, 929 girls per 1,000 boys [S1, indicator 4]. Also leave and pay.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** naming ceremonies; in many regions the mother goes to her parents' home for the birth. Write the hospital, the nights and the leave.
- **Risk:** Low to Medium. Sex selection is a crime; keep to "everyone hoped for a boy" talk at most.
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: home and family; work.

### M06. A parent's night in hospital
- **Who goes through it:** about one household in five each year. LASI Wave 1 (2017-18): 21% of households with a member aged 45 or over had someone admitted to hospital in the last 12 months [S6]. NFHS-5: 41.0% of households have any member covered by health insurance [S1, indicator 12].
- **Decisions about people:** the night rota (Split); who talks to the doctor (Pick one); who handles the money and the forms (Pick one); whose account of the symptoms the doctor trusts (Believe); who is asked to give blood (Rank). The shaadi demo's Day 4 used this and it worked.
- **Hard topic inside:** the care burden. Time Use Survey 2024: 41% of women and 21.4% of men aged 15 to 59 cared for household members on the survey day, for about 140 and 74 minutes [S2]. Also: who gives up work, and the cost.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** a government or a private hospital; distance to a city. Write the ward, the bench, the 2 am shift, not the size of the bill.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Rukavat or Lugdi (the night rush). Area: home and family.

### M07. Who looks after the ageing parents
- **Who goes through it:** nearly every adult child, sooner or later. LASI: of people 60 and over, 41% live with a spouse and children, 28% with children and no spouse, 20% with a spouse only, 6% alone (9% of older women) [S6, living arrangements]. Pew: about four in ten say sons should mainly care for ageing parents, 2% say daughters; 58% say both [S4].
- **Decisions about people:** which of two siblings, a daughter and a son in the same position, takes the parents in (Pick one; twin-friendly); who moves back to the home town; who pays; whose account of what the parent wants is believed.
- **Hard topic inside:** the care burden; inheritance ("the one who cares gets the house").
- **What varies, and how to write it:** joint and nuclear homes; how much daughters are expected to do (Pew finds Punjab far less traditional than the national figure [S4]). Write it as siblings deciding together.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: home and family; trust.

### M08. A death in the family: the days after
- **Who goes through it:** every family. Registered deaths were 86.5 lakh in 2022 and 102.2 lakh in 2021 [S7].
- **Decisions about people:** who calls the relatives; who deals with the death certificate, the bank and the pension (Pick one); who hosts the mourners and cooks (Split); who speaks for the family; who stays with the surviving parent.
- **Hard topic inside:** inheritance, and who performs the rites. Pew: 63% say sons should be mainly responsible for parents' last rites; 74% of Muslims and 29% of Sikhs; 81% in Karnataka and 30% in Kerala [S4]. (A news summary of the same survey gave Kerala as 40%; Pew's own page says 30%, which is why raw pages were read.)
- **What varies, and how to write it:** cremation or burial; 13 days or 40 days; food customs. Write the paperwork and the full house in the week after. Never show the rite.
- **Risk:** High if any rite is on screen; Low if the scene is the week after. The tone must stay gentle.
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: home and family; trust.

### M09. Dividing the family house or land
- **Who goes through it:** most families with property, eventually. NFHS-5: 43.3% of women aged 15 to 49 own a house or land alone or jointly [S1, indicator 121]. Pew: 64% say sons and daughters should have equal inheritance rights, yet many more give sons the greater claim than daughters [S4].
- **Decisions about people:** who is trusted to hold the papers (Pick one); whose claim is believed (Believe); who gets the house and who gets cash.
- **Hard topic inside:** inheritance; "she already got her share at her wedding" (a situation).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** inheritance law differs by religion; land, a shop or a flat. Write a family conversation with no legal claims on screen.
- **Risk:** Medium.
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: trust; home and family.

### M10. The chores at home, and the day everything goes wrong
- **Who goes through it:** everyone, every day [S2].
- **Decisions about people:** who does what this week (Split); who gets up when the water comes; who books the gas cylinder; who stays home when the help or the cook does not come.
- **Hard topic inside:** the care burden; "he is studying, leave him".
- **What varies, and how to write it:** paid help is urban and middle class; rural homes fetch water and fuel. Write the job, not the servant.
- **Risk:** Low, unless the scene centres on a maid (class-coded).
- **World fit:** Rukavat (the current Split). Area: home and family.

### M11. Water day: the tanker, the tap, the drums
- **Who goes through it:** many, not all. From a search summary only (NSS 76th round, 2018; the report was not opened): about 42.9% of rural households use a hand pump as the main drinking-water source and about 40.9% of urban households have piped water into the dwelling. Check before use.
- **Decisions about people:** who queues and carries (Split); who argues with the tanker man (Pick one); the building's water rota (Pick a team).
- **Hard topic inside:** the care burden; a quarrel at the tap (bullying).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** homes with water all day will not recognise it. Use it in dailies, not as a First take chapter.
- **Risk:** Medium (class and region).
- **World fit:** Station or Rukavat. Area: home and family; teams and leaders.

### M12. Monsoon flood or a long power cut
- **Who goes through it:** very many. CEEW's 2020 household survey: the average household got 20.6 hours of grid power a day, and two-thirds of rural and two-fifths of urban households have an outage at least once a day [S21]. About 40 million hectares are counted as flood-prone, on an estimate from 1980 [S22, secondary].
- **Decisions about people:** who goes out in the rain for medicines (Pick one); who checks the shop or the car; who stays with the elders and children (Split); which neighbour gets the spare key (Believe); the team to clear the drain (Pick a team).
- **Hard topic inside:** safety (who goes out at night); the care burden.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** floods hit Assam, Bihar, Mumbai, Chennai and Kerala hardest; elsewhere it is the heat or the cold. Write "the street is under water" or "the lights went at 8 pm".
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Lugdi (a pulp rescue night). Area: safety and justice; teams and leaders.

### M13. The year everyone stayed home (2020)
- **Who goes through it:** every adult.
- **Decisions about people:** who works where at home; who goes out for groceries; who nurses the sick; who teaches the children; whose job goes first.
- **Hard topic inside:** violence at home. The National Commission for Women's domestic violence complaints rose from 30 to 69 between the first and the last week of March 2020 [S24, secondary]. Also job loss and the care burden.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** a migrant worker's walk home and a flat with work from home are very different years.
- **Risk:** High (grief from 2021, and politics). Not a chapter; at most a passing memory.
- **World fit:** none suggested. Area: home and family; work.

### M14. A relative asks for a loan
- **Who goes through it:** many households. AIDIS 2019: about 35% of rural and 22.4% of urban households were in debt; 10.2% of rural households owed only non-institutional lenders, such as moneylenders, friends and family [S16].
- **Decisions about people:** which of two cousins with the same record to lend to (Pick one; twin-friendly); who keeps the family's cash; who stands guarantee; whose promise is believed (Believe).
- **Hard topic inside:** money and control; pressure on a widow; "women can't handle money" talk.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** amounts scale with class; keep them relative ("two months' salary").
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Jadugar (the trick of trust) or Rukavat. Area: trust.

### M15. A big buy on EMI: a phone, a scooter, a fridge
- **Who goes through it:** nearly every household. TRAI: 1,321.31 million telephone connections at the end of February 2026 [S17]. NFHS-5: 54.0% of women aged 15 to 49 have a mobile phone they use themselves (69.4% urban, 46.6% rural) [S1, indicator 123].
- **Decisions about people:** who gets the new phone when a son and a daughter both start college (Pick one; twin-friendly); who gets the scooter for the commute; whose name goes on the loan; who is believed about who broke the old one.
- **Hard topic inside:** control and safety (a daughter's phone checked; harassing calls).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** brands and prices; keep both off screen.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Lugdi or Rukavat. Area: trust; home and family.

## Work

### M16. A first job, or hiring someone
- **Who goes through it:** most adults, as the person looking or the person hiring. PLFS 2023-24: 60.1% of people 15 and over are in the labour force, 78.8% of men and 41.7% of women [S18, secondary]. Pew: 80% agree men should have more right to a job when jobs are scarce [S4].
- **Decisions about people:** you hire for a shop, a pharmacy, a clinic, a school or a call centre (Pick one, Rank); the family decides whose offer to take.
- **Hard topic inside:** the pay gap; "will she leave after marriage?"; a harassment complaint at work.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** formal and informal work. Use small workplaces everyone has stood in.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Station (the roster) or Jadugar (the troupe hiring). Area: work.

### M17. The government job exam in the family
- **Who goes through it:** very many families. For one railway recruitment in 2019, about 1.26 crore people registered for 35,208 posts [S19, secondary].
- **Decisions about people:** whose coaching the family pays for when a brother and a sister have the same marks (Pick one; twin-friendly); who travels with whom to a far exam centre; who gets the quiet room.
- **Hard topic inside:** money; safety on the journey to the centre; family pressure.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** railway, SSC, bank, police and state exams are known everywhere; the coaching town differs.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Station. Area: work; home and family.

### M18. Board exams and the choice of stream
- **Who goes through it:** nearly every family with children. A Ministry of Education analysis of 59 boards counted about 18.5 million Class 10 and 15.5 million Class 12 candidates; science was taken by 43%, mostly boys, and arts by 39%, more of them girls; an official added that more girls than boys sit the exams from government schools, which may show parents spending less on daughters' schooling [S9, secondary].
- **Decisions about people:** which child gets the private coaching (Pick one; twin-friendly); science or arts for a daughter and a son with the same marks; who is sent to a coaching city; who gets the quiet room; home chores in exam month (Split).
- **Hard topic inside:** pressure and bullying; spending on sons over daughters.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** the board and the language; the shape is the same everywhere.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Rukavat or Station. Area: home and family; work.

### M19. The family shop or farm in its busiest week
- **Who goes through it:** many, mostly rural. Time Use Survey 2024: 24.6% of rural people aged 15 to 59 produced goods for their own household's use on the survey day, for about 121 minutes [S2]. No figure for family shops was found.
- **Decisions about people:** who minds the till (Pick one); who goes to the market; who is trusted with the keys; who will run the shop one day.
- **Hard topic inside:** inheritance; women's work counted as "helping".
- **What varies, and how to write it:** a crop or a shop. Default to the shop, which city players also know.
- **Risk:** Medium (salaried city players stand further from it).
- **World fit:** Lugdi (the truck to the market) or Station. Area: work; trust.

### M20. A work team at a deadline
- **Who goes through it:** narrower: people in offices, factories and sites, and their families second-hand.
- **Decisions about people:** who goes on the client trip, who works the night shift, who presents, who is promoted (Pick a team, Rank).
- **Hard topic inside:** a harassment complaint; pay; a mother passed over.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** class.
- **Risk:** Low for community; reach narrower. Better for the dailies.
- **World fit:** Station (the current Station style). Area: work; teams and leaders.

## Safety and justice

### M21. The family train journey for an occasion
- **Who goes through it:** nearly everyone. Indian Railways carried 6,730 million originating passengers in 2023-24 (provisional) [S8].
- **Decisions about people:** who takes which berth (Rank); who guards the luggage; who gets off for food at the station (Pick one); who travels with whom; whose complaint the ticket checker believes (Believe).
- **Hard topic inside:** harassment while travelling. In one Delhi survey of about 1,400 people, more than 85% of women named buses, trains, stops and taxis as likely places for it [S23].
- **What varies, and how to write it:** places without trains go by bus (the hill states, much of the North-East). Write "the overnight journey" so either fits.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Station. Area: safety and justice; teams and leaders.

### M22. Coming home late
- **Who goes through it:** every family with young adults. The same Delhi survey: about 73% of women do not feel as free as men to use public spaces [S23] (Delhi only).
- **Decisions about people:** a brother and a sister both want to go to the late show or the night class (Pick one; twin-friendly); who is sent to fetch whom; whose late return is questioned (Believe).
- **Hard topic inside:** safety; control that calls itself care.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** city or village; the pattern is the same.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Lugdi (the night road) or Station. Area: safety and justice; home and family.

### M23. A theft or a quarrel in the building or the lane
- **Who goes through it:** common; no national number found.
- **Decisions about people:** whose account is believed: the watchman, a neighbour, the domestic worker, a teenager (Believe); who speaks to the police (Pick one).
- **Hard topic inside:** a harassment complaint in the building; blaming the poorest person. Caste and religion stay out until later levels, as the owner ruled.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** a housing society, a mohalla or a village lane.
- **Risk:** Medium (class).
- **World fit:** Jadugar (who is telling the truth) or Lugdi. Area: safety and justice; trust.

### M24. A bullying or ragging complaint at school or college
- **Who goes through it:** every family with a child in school. UDISE+ 2023-24: 24.8 crore school students [S10, secondary].
- **Decisions about people:** whose complaint is believed (Believe); who is made class monitor (Pick one); who goes to the meeting.
- **Hard topic inside:** bullying; a teacher's harassment (a situation only).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** little.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Jadugar or Cricket. Area: safety and justice.

### M25. A small road accident
- **Who goes through it:** very common; no national number was fetched.
- **Decisions about people:** whose version is believed when a woman and a man with the same driving record each say the other turned without looking (Believe; twin-friendly); who goes to the police station; who deals with the insurance.
- **Hard topic inside:** a police complaint; the "woman driver" joke.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** a scooter, a car or an auto.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Lugdi (the road). Area: safety and justice; trust.

### M26. Trouble in a neighbour's or a relative's home
- **Who goes through it:** many know of it closely. NFHS-5: 29.3% of ever-married women aged 18 to 49 have ever faced physical or sexual violence from a husband [S1, indicator 125].
- **Decisions about people:** whom you believe; whom you call; who goes over; who can stay with you.
- **Hard topic inside:** violence at home (a situation only, with the help line in "?").
- **What varies, and how to write it:** little.
- **Risk:** High in weight. Keep inside one decision; never a chapter frame.
- **World fit:** Cricket (the current police-complaint twin) or Rukavat. Area: safety and justice.

## Trust

### M27. Renting out a room or a flat
- **Who goes through it:** most people who move, and 28.9% of people counted as migrants in PLFS 2020-21 [S3]. No renting share was fetched.
- **Decisions about people:** you are the landlord choosing between a single working woman and a single working man with the same income (Pick one; twin-friendly); whose deposit comes back; who is believed about the damage (Believe).
- **Hard topic inside:** landlords refusing single women or single men. Religion and caste come later, as ruled.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** mostly a city moment; village players know the tenant upstairs too.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Lugdi or Rukavat. Area: trust.

### M28. Choosing someone to trust at home: a tutor, a driver, a cook, a babysitter
- **Who goes through it:** narrower: middle-class homes mostly.
- **Decisions about people:** Pick one; Believe.
- **Hard topic inside:** a child's safety; harassment.
- **Risk:** Medium (class-coded).
- **World fit:** Jadugar. Area: trust; safety and justice.

### M29. Meeting a family for a match
- **Who goes through it:** most families. In IHDS 2011-12, about 5% of women chose their husband with no family input, 55% had some say, and nearly 65% met him only at or around the wedding; self-choice reached 34% in the North-East [S15].
- **Decisions about people:** whose opinion counts; who is believed about the other family; whether her job or his job decides the city.
- **Hard topic inside:** dowry; consent; pressure to "adjust".
- **What varies, and how to write it:** love marriages in cities; the North-East differs. It folds best into M01.
- **Risk:** Medium (reads Hindu and North Indian when written with "rishta" detail).
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: home and family; trust.

## Teams and leaders

### M30. Gully cricket or a local tournament
- **Who goes through it:** nearly everyone, as player or crowd. The 2023 World Cup final had a peak TV audience of 130 million, and 300 million watched it on one network's TV channels in India alone; women were 34% of viewers [S11].
- **Decisions about people:** pick the team (Pick a team); the batting order; the captain; who bowls the last over (the shaadi demo's Day 1).
- **Hard topic inside:** bullying (a girl who plays gets teased); harassment at the ground.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** football in Kerala, Goa, Bengal and the North-East; cricket is still known there.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Cricket. Area: teams and leaders.

### M31. Watching the big match together
- **Who goes through it:** as M30 [S11].
- **Decisions about people:** who gets the TV, the best seat, the snacks run; who cooks through the match (Split).
- **Hard topic inside:** the care burden [S2].
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Cricket or Rukavat (the family TV). Area: home and family; teams and leaders.

### M32. The society, mohalla or panchayat committee
- **Who goes through it:** most adults, as members or neighbours. Over 2.7 lakh gram panchayats serve nearly 65% of the population, with about 25 lakh elected members, 49.75% of them women [S20].
- **Decisions about people:** who becomes secretary or treasurer (Pick one, Rank); whose plan for the water problem is believed (Believe); who leads the cleaning drive (Pick a team); who speaks to the officials.
- **Hard topic inside:** a husband acting for his elected wife (a situation); harassment of a woman member.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** a city society or a village panchayat; both are familiar.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Station or Jadugar. Area: teams and leaders.

### M33. The function: school annual day, Independence Day, the society show
- **Who goes through it:** nearly everyone through school [S10]; national days belong to no one community.
- **Decisions about people:** who hosts on stage; who leads the dance or play (Pick one); who handles the money; the running order (Rank, Batting order).
- **Hard topic inside:** bullying; harassment backstage (the current Jadugar topic).
- **What varies, and how to write it:** very little.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Jadugar (the stage). Area: teams and leaders.

### M34. The parents' meeting at school
- **Who goes through it:** every family with a school child [S10]. Pew: 34% say child care should be mainly women's, 62% both [S4].
- **Decisions about people:** which parent goes when both work (Pick one; twin-friendly); who helps with homework (Split); whose report about the child the teacher believes.
- **Hard topic inside:** who the children live with after a separation (both parents come); bullying.
- **What varies, and how to write it:** little.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **World fit:** Rukavat. Area: home and family; work.

Three ways a fresh First take could hold these together (seedlings, not proposals):
1. **One family, one year.** Five chapters across a present-day year in one family: exam month, a summer train journey to a wedding, a monsoon night, the big match, a hospital week. The player is the one everyone calls.
2. **One lane, five homes.** Each chapter is a different household in the same lane or building. This widens the cast and keeps each person in one world and one role, as the owner asked on 23 Oct.
3. **Five favours.** Five people the player knows each ask for help with one familiar event. Each chapter opens on the ask in a cutscene; no group chat.

## Evidence on familiar scenarios and subtle measures

1. **Familiar situations give truer answers.** Aguinis and Bradley's best-practice guide for scenario studies says the situation shown must "be familiar to them, otherwise responses may be artificial" [S25]. The same guide says lifelike scenes can carry everyday "noise" from distractors without harming the measure, as long as the designer controls that noise. That supports decoy notes and remarks.
2. **Prior experience draws people into a story.** Green (2004): readers with knowledge or experience related to a story's themes felt more drawn into it, being drawn in went with finding the story realistic, and those drawn in held more story-consistent beliefs afterwards [S26]. One story, 152 US students, correlation only.
3. **Context and personal detail raise engagement.** Cordova and Lepper (1996): putting a learning game into a context, making it personal, and giving choices each raised motivation and engagement [S27]. Children aged 9 to 11 in a maths game; a direction, not proof for adults.
4. **Side-by-side choices match real behaviour best.** Hainmueller, Hangartner and Yamamoto (2015) compared survey choices with real Swiss votes on who gets citizenship, a topic where people hide prejudice. Survey effects matched the real votes closely, and the design showing two people side by side came closest, within about 2 percentage points on average [S28]. Retake's cards with a hidden pair work the same way.
5. **Hide the sensitive detail by spreading it.** Walzenbach (2019): when the sensitive detail changed only between respondents rather than within one person's set, it drew less attention and suited reducing answers given to look good [S29]. For Retake: keep a twin's two halves far apart, and in the dailies the swapped half can come on another day.
6. **"You" for everyday choices; someone else's case for hard ones.** Kleinewiese (preprint): scenarios written to "you" generally gave better data because they draw people in, while for sensitive topics a third-person case may be better, though less clearly; third person also primed later answers less [S30].
7. **Guessing the purpose matters less than feared.** Mummolo and Peterson (2019): telling over 12,000 participants what the researchers expected did not change the results, and even paying them to comply did not reliably shift them [S31]. But that is about knowing the purpose. A visible cue that marks the pair in every task, as the opening remarks did in 32 of 40 decisions (team review, 23 Oct 2026), is a different problem, and a scored game gives players a reason to look fair. So remove the giveaways, but secrecy does not need to be total.
8. **Leads not opened (check before use).** Eifler and Petzold (2019) report limits on how well scenario answers match real behaviour; Moeller and Petzold (2026 conference abstract) reportedly find answers drift furthest from behaviour in high-norm, high-cost situations such as hiring. Both were seen only in search summaries; the pages were blocked or not reached.

## Seedlings for ground rules (from the catalogue and the evidence)

For the First take:
- Present day only. Every chapter is a moment most adults have lived or watched closely. Check each one by asking: would a player in Guwahati, Kochi, Lucknow and a Vidarbha village all say "I have been there"?
- Write what everyone shares (logistics, people, time, money) and keep ritual, dress and community words off screen.
- Second person ("you pick") for everyday choices; hard topics arrive as someone else's case the player judges.
- The hard topic sits inside a moment as a situation, never as the chapter's frame; never on the player's own family member.
- Twins come from moments that hand a woman and a man the same position (two siblings, two applicants, two cousins, two parents), with the halves far apart and among other changing details.

For the dailies:
- A daily may name a festival, a region or a custom, as long as these rotate across the year and across communities.
- Narrower moments (office deadlines, hired help, water queues, the family farm) belong here, not in the First take.
- Moments with high emotional weight (a death, violence at home, the lockdown year) appear only as single decisions with the help line, never as a whole puzzle's theme.
