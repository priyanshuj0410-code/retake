# Saal Bhar (साल भर, All Year): story bible for the blend

**Status.** A brief for writers, built on the blend (options file A4) and the owner's five answers of 25 Oct. Not scored, not checked, not painted. Rule numbers are the rulebook's (ground-rules-draft-2026-10-25.md). "Rulebook Q" and "options Q" name the two question lists.

**The owner's five answers, as this bible applies them.**
1. Score the blend: this is the blend, with only the changes listed in section 14, so the judges score what was proposed.
2. The player is the grown-up child of the house, 22 to 30, never painted, no gender, caste or religion (rule 8). The elder is now a grandparent, so she is **Nani**.
3. Scroll strip: each chapter is one strip; each decision is a stop in it (section 5).
4. Just the look: each world is only the painting style of a present-day chapter (rule 49). The blend's world for each chapter keeps today's world-area pairs, so the results screen needs no change.
5. The owner judges the rules through the story. **Writers mark, never fix silently.** After any beat that breaks a rule, write `[BREAKS rule N: why]`. After any beat that waits on an owner answer, write `[WAITS rulebook Q9]` (or options Q). The beats this bible already marks are in section 14.

## 1. Title

**Saal Bhar** (साल भर, "all year"). One household's year, held together by a wall calendar; plain spoken Hindi that reads the same in Hinglish; no gender; no year number on screen (rule 2). It was already the options file's candidate (options Q10).

How it was checked: three web searches on 25 Oct 2026 (film, series, show or app named "Saal Bhar"; then Google Play and the App Store) found no title of that name, only unrelated ones such as "Saala". That search runs on a US web index, so four checks remain before it is used: the Play Store's and App Store's own search, the Indian trademark register, and regional film titles. The phrase is ordinary Hindi, so nobody can own it in plain use, but a registered mark in games would still block it. It names the First take inside Retake, not the app.

## 2. The player

- **Who.** The grown-up child of the house, 22 to 30, the one everyone calls. No name, no face, no hands, no shadow (rule 7). People look out of the panel at "you" and hold things out towards you.
- **What people call you.** "You" (तुम to family, the game's own voice आप) or the job: "in charge" (इंचार्ज). Never bhaiya, didi, beta, or any word that fixes gender (rule 8). Elders use no family word for you until rulebook Q7 is answered.
- **What you call them.** Nani, Mummy, Papa, Mama ji, Tinku; in one chapter each, Mausi, Mausa ji, Masterji, Captain sahab, Mami. Every one of these is said by daughters and sons alike.
- **Your "You" bubbles.** Two or three a chapter, one tap each, the same whatever you will pick, never a choice, never measured, never naming a card person, never agreeing with a remark (rule 38). Tail off the bottom edge, tag "You" | "आप", never yellow.
- **Hindi forms that mark no gender** (rule 66; the checker flags, a native reader clears):
  - To elders, polite commands: लिस्ट दिखाइए, चलिए, आप आराम कीजिए, नानी। To Tinku: स्कोर बताओ।
  - Offers in the subjunctive: मैं देखूँ? Impersonal: हो जाएगा। (flagged and cleared, as in the strip sample).
  - Never: मैं कर लूँगा/लूँगी, मैं आ गया/गई, थका/थकी हूँ.
  - Lines to you: commands (सुनो, देखो, चुनो), "who should" asks in the subjunctive (कौन जाए? never कौन जाएगा?), and lines where you are not the doer: आज इंचार्ज तुम हो। फ़ैसला तुम्हारा। लिस्ट तुम्हारे हाथ में। सब तुम्हें ही फ़ोन करते हैं।

## 5. The strip, the same in every chapter

From the scroll design and the judges' fixes, in reading order:
1. **Title card**: "Chapter n of 5", the title, one trouble line, the hard topic's setting named plainly (rule 26), "About 4 minutes", Start.
2. **The ask**: the asker sets the stakes in one beat, then hands over the **list of targets** (2 targets, in the same words as the card numbers), shown as live text on a painted blank paper. It comes back stamped at the end.
3. **The circle page**: the ten card people as their own card portraits, names only, one size (at least 56 px), new order on every load, over a painted place with no people. No relation tags, no quirks, no "All younger than you" (rule 8's clash note).
4. **Scenes**: one lead-in per scene after a long gutter, a narration box with the clock and the place ("9:40 pm. The junction."). The last panel before each stop is the asker giving the goal of the moment, a time or a need, never the skill the two tie on (rule 37).
5. **Stops** (decisions): the strip halts; the question sits in the asker's balloon at the fixed place, or in the plain question box on a hard decision; behind the cards, a people-free painting of the job's place; the story clock in the top bar. After the pick: a settled plate (tick, job word, lock, no names, no faces), then an "it happened" panel only at a change of place, the same for every pick.
6. **Closing**: the event painting with the stamp in the world's big moment (Lugdi's cover reprint, Station's split-flap tiles, Jadugar's crimson cloth, Cricket's "decision pending", Rukavat's interruption card), never on a hard decision (rule 25); the list stamped; one warm reaction from the asker about the event, never a person; your fixed reply; the story's own fixed ending, which leads into the next chapter; then the five circles.

Standing rules for every writer: no bubble names, points at or uses the note words of anyone on the coming cards (rule 36); both halves of a twin have the same asker, the same number and shape of bubbles, words within 2, and matching lead-in paintings (rule 37); no reaction to any pick until the chapter's end (rule 39); your reply after a pick names only the job ("Berths, sorted"), never the person `[WAITS options Q14]`; Back on story pages; Skip never jumps past the goal of the moment; a painted phone or paper too small for its words becomes a message card under the panel (rule 40).

**Words** (write to the proposed caps; rulebook Q14 decides whether speech counts): a spoken line at most 10 words, narration 12, a beat 20, a chapter's story 120, the opening 80, each language counted alone; a question at most 10 words and a decision screen 15 (rule 63). Every line in Hinglish, Hindi and English (rule 65) `[WAITS rulebook Q6]` for what a Hinglish bubble looks like.
