What works

  • The opening places you inside 40 words: Nani's "Everyone comes", Mummy's free calendar from the shop, "Everyone calls you anyway", the pen.
  • Being the grown-up child everyone phones works for every player, the 61-year-old included, and nobody ever says your age.
  • One chapter leads to the next all year: the wet step, the ward, the July circle, the cracked drain, the drain as the cup's stake, the cake box.
  • The endings are clean: swapping the hidden pair, or always picking the woman or always the man, never changes an outcome.
  • No Hindi line to you marks you as a woman or a man, and the household never sits on a card.

The six big problems

  1. Too long. 162 beats, about 150 new paintings, 25 to 32 minutes against 20, and about 2,260 words on screen.
  2. The numbers give the pair away. In 38 of 40 decisions the hidden pair are the only two cards with matching numbers. It is built in: with 10 people on fixed numbers, the two left over are always a woman and a man.
  3. Asks nobody would make. The family shop hands its cash drawer to people hired that week; Nani's spare key goes to neighbours while you stand there.
  4. The cards read like a poster. Against-type jobs on nearly every card, chapter after chapter, which tells players what is being tested.
  5. The strip shows a clock, not people. The person asking is always seen from behind, and most "it happened" beats are an empty room with a sound.
  6. Chapter 4's logic breaks. Players are in two matches at once, and "Our side, all ten" is followed by picking three more.

What the story says about the rules

  • Some rules fight the structure, not the writer. "The pair is never the only tie" cannot hold with circles of 10 people on fixed numbers; chapters 2, 4 and 5 broke it on every decision. The way out is bigger circles (12 people), numbers that change per decision, or dropping that rule.
  • The 20 minutes and 8 decisions a chapter cannot both hold in a strip. Six decisions a chapter, one painting per scene rather than per stop, or accepting about 25 minutes.
  • "The household is painted only in today's style" is what empties the strip. Painting each chapter's asker face-on in that chapter's look (five reference paintings) brings the people back.
  • "Ask only what someone in your place would be asked" caught real problems, and so did "nothing else that follows gender sits on the same side in both halves" (the most broken rule, 15 times). Both are worth keeping.

Four questions this raises

  1. Paint the person asking face-on in each chapter's look?
  2. Shorter (6 decisions a chapter, about 20 minutes), or keep 8 and accept about 25?
  3. For the numbers: 12 people a chapter, numbers that change per decision, or drop "never the only tie"?
  4. Mostly ordinary jobs on the cards, with against-type only now and then?
