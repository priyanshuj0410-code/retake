How a decision works

  1. 3 to 5 people, one question, pick then confirm. Cards look the same before a choice and come in a new order for each player.
  2. Two of them have exactly the same numbers. One is a woman, one a man. Nothing else on their cards differs except one thing, called the nudge here.
  3. The nudge is the one thing that leans one way: the words the speaker uses about them, a voice pushing for or against one, a family detail, a hard fact said of one of them (a complaint of bullying they made, a report doubted, a police complaint against them, a wish to marry again, saying "no dowry"), or a detail that really matters to the job (a section learned, a licence held, who saw it, dust that makes one ill, who keeps wicket).
  4. Every decision has a twin, four decisions later in the same chapter, with a different two people and the nudge on the other side: on the man if it was on the woman, and the other way round. The two halves of a twin share no person, so liking or disliking one face cannot read as a lean. Both halves sit in the same chapter, so a chapter is complete on its own.
  5. How a twin reads.
    • The woman both times: a lean towards women.
    • The man both times: a lean towards men.
    • Following the nudge both times: no lean from this twin.
    • Picking outside the matching two: no reading from that decision.
    • "Towards the men" always means the men got the seat, the run, the trust, the benefit of the doubt or the lighter share. Both directions count exactly the same.
  6. Nudges that matter. One twin in each chapter has a nudge that really matters. There, following it both times is the player's own sound rule, and simply giving the woman (or the man) the same answer both times no longer looks steady. This is what stops "always the same answer" from passing.
  7. Hard twins. Each chapter also has one hard twin (Rukavat has two), never the twin whose nudge matters: it carries the chapter's hard topic. It reads like any other twin: what counts is whether the hard fact weighs the same on a woman as on a man. Holding it against both, or against neither, is no lean from that twin; holding it against one side only is a lean. Only the speaker ever says a slanted thing about it, and the hard fact is never said of a name chosen to mark a community. Suggested: result cards 2 and 3 never draw on a hard twin, so no one is told "you picked the man both times" about a police complaint.
  8. The Kind column. Each chapter's twin table names every twin's kind: "matters" for the twin whose nudge really matters, "hard" for the hard topic, and "plain" for the other two. So every chapter has one matters twin, one hard twin and two plain twins, except Rukavat, which has two hard twins and one plain.
  9. The lean figure itself is the research line's. This draft only fixes what each decision can show. How the 20 twins become one number, and how sure it is, waits on the research line.
