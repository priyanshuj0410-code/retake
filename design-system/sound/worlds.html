Sound for the new worlds

Music and sound effects for Jadugar, Cricket and Rukavat, built on the rules you set on the first sound page. Things to hear and compare. You decide.

Every sound here is made live in your browser. No audio files are downloaded. Sound starts only when you press a button.

Before you press Play

  • The first press wakes the sound. Browsers do not allow it earlier.
  • Start with the volume low, as set. If you hear nothing, check your phone's silent switch and media volume.
  • One music loop plays at a time. The sound effects play over it, so you can hear them together.
  • Try a cheap phone speaker too. That is how many players will hear it.
  • Like the game, this page fades the music out after 3 minutes without a tap.

The one rule: sound never hints at a lean

No sound may tell a player that a pick was good or bad, or which way it leaned. Every sketch below was checked against this first.

  • No "right" or "wrong" sounds, no cheer, no groan, no applause.
  • Each sound is a fixed recipe: the same notes, length and loudness for every person, every pick, every day. None of them can even receive the score or the lean.
  • The big moments are part of the result, so they follow this too: the cloth, the scoreboard and the interruption card sound the same after a score of 12 as after 90.

no beat · loop 16 s

The hum of the floodlights, a crowd murmuring far away with no words and no cheering, and twice a loop a bat meeting a ball across the ground, with its echo off the empty stands.

  • Far bat

Fit

Risk

6All the sound effects

The same twelve events as Lugdi and Station, so every world covers the whole game. Each is a fixed recipe of time only: it cannot receive who you picked, the score or the lean. Play them over a music loop to hear the mix.

The Send and the one vibration pulse

As in Lugdi and Station: the Send's sound and the single short pulse start on the same tick, and nothing else ever vibrates. The pulse does not depend on the sound switch. Phones with no vibration, iPhones in the browser among them, get the sound alone.

What calm sound changes in each world

  • Jadugar: the reveal is one short brush and the settle; the milestone is the card slap only; the music loses the cymbals, the dugdugi, the banjo's quick repeats and the glass echo.
  • Cricket: the reveal is one plate; the milestone is the plate only; the music loses the far bat, the static, the radio lead and the crease scrape.
  • Rukavat: the card sits still with a shorter tone, then cuts; the milestone is the shutter knock only; the music plays single santoor notes, with no rolls, no echo, no clinks and no static.
  • Every loop also sits a little lower.

7In public, and evoking without copying

What could startle someone nearby

  • Left out of every world: cheers, groans, applause, crowd roars, horns, whistles, sirens, doorbells, a ringing phone, the pressure cooker's whistle, a TV test tone and any spoken words.
  • Jadugar's dugdugi on the cover is a quick little roll. It is soft and lasts under half a second, but it is the most likely sound in this world to make someone look.
  • Cricket's Send is a bat's tock. A sharp crack carries in a quiet room, so its edge is rounded off and it is no louder than the other worlds' Send.
  • Rukavat's card tone plays on every result, and the card is an announcer's card by nature. It is one soft, round note, never a beep, but it is the closest thing here to an announcement (question 3).
  • Static in Rukavat's cover sound and Cricket's radio is a rush of noise. Both are short and soft. Loud static startles, and it can make people think their phone is broken.

Evoke, never copy

  • Every tune on this page is new, written for this page. There are no recordings and no samples.
  • Jadugar: no snake charmer's pipe, and nothing like the famous snake-charmer tune from old films.
  • Cricket: nothing like any sports channel's theme, any radio station's signature tune or any commentator's voice.
  • Rukavat: nothing like the old national channel's opening tune or its logo sound. The card's tone is one plain note of our own. No film songs and no film background music.
  • Instruments and scales are shared heritage: a santoor, a dholak or an evening scale belongs to no one. A melody can belong to someone, so each melody here should still get a quick listen from someone who knows the old tunes well before launch.

8For you to decide

  1. Do the three suggested picks suit you?

    Example: in a Jadugar week, plucked strings play quietly under the puzzle and the cover gets the dugdugi. In a Cricket week, a quiet radio tune. In a Rukavat week, the santoor.

    Options: the suggestions; another direction in a world; or no music under the puzzle in that world, only its cover sound, as in Station.

    We suggest: Jadugar B, Cricket B and Rukavat A, each trimmed. Cricket and Rukavat have tunes, Lugdi does not, so you may want one of them quieter still.

  2. Should Cricket's scoreboard always hang three plates?

    Example: today a score of 86 hangs two plates and 100 hangs three. If each plate knocks, a high score would sound different from a low one, and the result must sound the same every time.

    Options: always hang three plates, with blank ones in front of a short score, as old hand-hung boards did; or keep the drawing and play three knocks whatever it shows.

    We suggest: always three plates. Then the picture and the sound match every day. This changes Cricket's scoreboard drawing.

  3. May Rukavat's interruption card have its soft tone?

    Example: the world is named after an announcer's card, and your rule says nothing announcement-like in the game. The sketch gives the card one soft, round note on every result.

    Options: the soft tone; or no tone, only the projector, the cut and the picture warming up.

    We suggest: the soft tone. It is the card's whole character, it is soft and round, never a beep, and it is nothing like a station chime or a bell.

  4. Should the Jadugar cloth end with a magician's flourish?

    Example: a little "ta-da" as the cloth comes off feels like magic. But it plays the same after a score of 12 as after 90, and after 12 it may feel like a joke at the player's expense.

    Options: the plain swish and settle; or with the flourish.

    We suggest: the plain swish. Sound says "we heard you", never "well done".

  5. Should the First take play music?

    Example: the First take runs 20 minutes over five chapters, each in its own world, so the music would change about every 4 minutes.

    Options: each chapter plays its world's music; sound effects only, no music; or one world's music for the whole First take.

    We suggest: sound effects only. Its result, the one screen that shows which way you lean, plays the same sound for everyone, like any day's result.

How the sketches are made
  • The same engine as the first sound page: plain Web Audio, with oscillators, one shared noise buffer, filters, gain envelopes and a simple echo. No samples, no files, no outside scripts.
  • One audio context, created on your first press. Everything runs through a master compressor and the volume control, so nothing clips.
  • Every sound is mono, so one speaker or one earbud loses nothing.
  • Loops are scheduled a fraction of a second ahead, so the beat stays steady when the page is busy. One loop plays at a time.
  • Where a sound has a scattered pattern, such as the fringe beads or the static crackles, the pattern is fixed, so it is identical every time.
  • Music stops when this page is hidden or the screen locks, and fades after 3 minutes without a tap.
  • These are sketches for judging an idea. Final levels should be tuned on a cheap Android phone speaker and on earphones.
